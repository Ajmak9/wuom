# 1. Purpose

The **Climate Tree** organizes the reading of climatic conditions that influence a territory.

It does not attempt to predict the future with certainty.

It does not replace meteorology or climate science.

Its role is to integrate climate signals, trends, extreme events and uncertainty in order to guide situated decisions for adaptation, care and territorial return.

---

# 2. Central Question

> **Which climatic conditions are changing the functioning of the territory?**

---

# 3. What It Observes

The Climate Tree observes:

- temperature;
- precipitation;
- drought;
- humidity;
- wind;
- snow and ice;
- extreme events;
- trends;
- variability;
- anomalies;
- climate uncertainty;
- emerging territorial signals.

---

# 4. Sources of Reading

The Climate Tree may integrate:

- weather stations;
- satellite and remote sensing data;
- climate models;
- citizen and local observation networks;
- situated knowledge;
- territorial experience;
- historical records;
- climate indicators;
- early warning systems.

No single source is sufficient.

Value emerges through their integration.

---

# 5. Time Scales

Climate must be interpreted across multiple temporal scales.

| Scale | Reading |
|--------|----------|
| Intra-annual | Days, weeks and specific events |
| Seasonal | Seasonal transitions and cycles |
| Interannual | Year-to-year variability |
| Decadal | Long-term trends |
| Extreme | Rare or exceptional events |

An isolated event is less informative than a sequence.

---

# 6. Main Components

## 6.1 Temperature

Observes:

- maximums;
- minimums;
- averages;
- thermal anomalies;
- persistence of heat.

Closely related to:

- human health;
- vegetation stress;
- energy demand;
- wildfire risk;
- water availability;
- thermal comfort.

---

## 6.2 Precipitation

Observes:

- amount;
- intensity;
- distribution;
- seasonality.

Rainfall matters not only because of how much falls, but also when and how it occurs.

---

## 6.3 Drought

Observes:

- water deficit;
- evapotranspiration;
- soil moisture deficit;
- real water availability.

Drought depends on rainfall, temperature, vegetation, soil and human demand.

---

## 6.4 Humidity

Observes:

- atmospheric humidity;
- soil moisture;
- vapor pressure deficit;
- nighttime recovery capacity.

Humidity strongly influences vegetation, wildfire risk and ecosystem resilience.

---

## 6.5 Wind

Observes:

- prevailing directions;
- intensity;
- gusts;
- abnormal events.

Wind affects evaporation, vegetation stress, wildfire behaviour and dust transport.

---

## 6.6 Snow and Ice

Observes:

- snow accumulation;
- melting;
- duration of snow cover;
- delayed water availability.

Essential for mountainous watersheds and seasonal water storage.

---

## 6.7 Extreme Events

Observes:

- storms;
- floods;
- heatwaves;
- cold waves;
- hail;
- strong winds;
- exceptional events.

Extreme events are interpreted through intensity, duration, frequency and cumulative effects.

---

## 6.8 Trends

Observes climatic evolution over different time horizons.

A trend is identified through sequences rather than isolated observations.

---

# 7. Climate Uncertainty

Climate is inherently non-linear.

The Climate Tree works with:

- ranges;
- scenarios;
- probabilities;
- uncertainty margins;
- emerging signals;
- plausible risks.

Uncertainty should improve orientation, not prevent action.

# 11. Core Principles

### Every territory has its own climate.

Global climate manifests differently according to relief, soils, vegetation, altitude and land use.

---

### Climate changes; understanding how and when matters most.

The sequence is often more informative than the event itself.

---

### The past helps us understand the present.

Historical records provide context for present observations.

---

### Anticipate without absolute certainty.

Adaptation requires informed decisions despite uncertainty.

---

### Climate is ultimately verified in the territory.

Models guide understanding.

Territorial return confirms reality.

---

# 12. Limits

The Climate Tree must not be used to:

- claim unsupported causal relationships;
- confuse isolated events with trends;
- replace official meteorological services;
- ignore uncertainty;
- separate climate from territory;
- present scenarios as certainties.

---

# 13. Custody Rule

Climate information should be handled carefully whenever it may affect:

- vulnerable species;
- sensitive locations;
- critical infrastructure;
- strategic water resources;
- exposed communities.

---

# 14. Core Statements

> Climate cannot be understood outside the territory.

> One climate signal is never enough.  
> Sequences provide orientation.

> Uncertainty does not prevent action.  
> It requires better orientation.

> Information may be global.  
> Orientation is always territorial.

> Without return, there is extraction.
