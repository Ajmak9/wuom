# 1. Purpose

The **Economy Tree** organizes the reading of the economic flows that influence a territory.

It does not study the economy as an isolated objective.

Its function is to understand how production, work, resources, investment, distribution, exchange and consumption affect the capacity of a territory to sustain life, well-being and long-term resilience.

---

# 2. Central Question

> **How does the economy influence the viability of a territory and its capacity to adapt?**

---

# 3. What It Observes

The Economy Tree observes:

- production;
- employment;
- resources;
- investment;
- consumption;
- distribution;
- exchange;
- added value;
- local economy;
- external dependency;
- economic vulnerability;
- sustainability;
- environmental costs;
- territorial resilience.

---

# 4. Sources of Reading

The Economy Tree may integrate:

- official statistics;
- local and regional economic accounts;
- business and cooperative records;
- participatory observation;
- interviews with key actors;
- market and value-chain analysis;
- sustainability indicators;
- territorial knowledge;
- historical records;
- economic mapping.

No single source is sufficient.

Economic understanding emerges through their integration with the territory.

# 11. Core Principles

### The economy is a means, not an end.

Economic activity should support life, territory and long-term continuity.

---

### Territory generates value.

Economic wealth also depends on soil, water, biodiversity, knowledge, health and community.

---

### Equity matters more than empty growth.

Growth without territorial balance weakens resilience.

---

### Sustainability before short-term profit.

Immediate gains that degrade ecological foundations create future losses.

---

### Without return, there is extraction.

Every economic activity should return value, care or learning to the territory that sustains it.

---

# 12. Limits

The Economy Tree must not be used to:

- justify extractive practices;
- reduce territory to monetary value;
- ignore ecological impacts;
- conceal inequality;
- replace democratic deliberation;
- equate growth with well-being.

The economy is always interpreted as part of a living territorial system.

---

# 13. Custody Rule

Economic information requires careful management whenever it involves:

- individuals;
- small businesses;
- financial data;
- vulnerable communities;
- strategic resources;
- territorial conflicts;
- ongoing negotiations.

---

# 14. Core Statements

> The economy does not exist above the territory. It depends on it.

> Value that never returns becomes extraction.

> Territorial wealth cannot be measured only in money.

> A resilient economy supports the life that supports it.

> Without return, there is extraction.
