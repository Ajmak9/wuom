18. BIODIVERSITY TREE v1.0
BIOIA / WUOM
Reading biodiversity to understand and protect living territories

The Biodiversity Tree organizes biodiversity as a living network rather than a list of species.

Biodiversity emerges from the relationships between ecosystems, habitats, species, ecological processes and genetic diversity. Together they sustain water, soils, climate, food systems and human well-being.

Within BIOIA / WUOM, biodiversity becomes a strategic layer for territorial reading, stewardship and ecological resilience.

Without biodiversity there is no resilience.

Reading dimensions

The Biodiversity Tree integrates five complementary dimensions:

Territory: ecosystems, habitats, ecological corridors and landscape connectivity.
Data: species records, habitat quality, ecological indicators and biodiversity datasets.
Experience: field observation, ecological knowledge and scientific expertise.
AI for reading: pattern recognition, habitat analysis and detection of ecological change.
Return: verified information that supports conservation, restoration and adaptive management.
Functions of the Biodiversity Tree
Read biodiversity as a living system.
Connect habitats, species and ecological processes.
Detect emerging ecological pressures.
Support conservation and restoration decisions.
Strengthen ecological resilience through verified knowledge.
Promote long-term stewardship of shared territories.
What it is not

The Biodiversity Tree is not:

a simple species inventory,
a surveillance platform,
an automated conservation system,
a substitute for ecological fieldwork,
artificial intelligence replacing scientific judgement.

Its purpose is to improve ecological understanding through integrated territorial reading.

BIOIA / WUOM orientation

Biodiversity cannot be understood by counting species alone.

It must be interpreted through relationships between ecosystems, territory, ecological processes and human activities.

Artificial intelligence helps organize information.

People provide ecological interpretation.

The territory provides verification.

Key idea

To understand biodiversity is to understand the living fabric that supports every territory.
