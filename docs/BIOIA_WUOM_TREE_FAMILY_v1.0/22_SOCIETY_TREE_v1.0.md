# 1. Purpose

The **Society Tree** organizes the reading of the human dimension of a territory.

It does not focus only on population statistics.

Its function is to understand how communities live, cooperate, adapt and shape the territory in order to support fair, resilient and situated decision-making.

---

# 2. Central Question

> **How does society respond to territorial conditions, and how does it influence the territory in return?**

---

# 3. What It Observes

The Society Tree observes:

- communities;
- health and well-being;
- housing;
- culture;
- identity;
- education;
- knowledge;
- participation;
- social cohesion;
- equity;
- inclusion;
- adaptation;
- resilience;
- vulnerability;
- local economy;
- relationships between people and territory.

# 4. Sources of Reading

The Society Tree may integrate:

- population statistics;

- local knowledge;
- social indicators;

Social understanding emerges through the integration of multiple perspectives.

# 11. Core Principles

### People are part of the territory.

Territory cannot exist without the communities that inhabit it.

---

### Listen before deciding.

Situated observation precedes intervention.

---

### Diversity strengthens resilience.

Plurality improves adaptation and learning.

---

### Participation improves governance.

Better decisions emerge from multiple perspectives.

---

### Without return, there is no learning.

Every intervention should return to the territory for verification.

---

# 12. Limits

The Society Tree must not be used for:

- mass surveillance;
- individual profiling;
- political manipulation;
- discrimination;
- extraction of personal data;
- replacing democratic dialogue.

Its purpose is to understand social systems, not to control people.

---

# 13. Custody Rule

Social information requires particular care.

Its use should always respect:

- privacy;
- anonymization;
- ethics;
- proportionality;
- protection of vulnerable groups.

---

# 14. Core Statements

> Listening is part of governance.
