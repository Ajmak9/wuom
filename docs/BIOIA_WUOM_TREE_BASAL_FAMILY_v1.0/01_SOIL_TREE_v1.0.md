## BIOIA / WUOM  
### Basal Family v1.0

---

## Purpose

The Soil Tree organizes the reading of soil as a living system within BIOIA / WUOM.

Its function is not only to observe soil as a physical surface, but to understand how it sustains life, stores memory, regulates water and supports ecological continuity.

The soil is not an inert support.

It is a living matrix of relationships.

---

## Core principle

**The soil sustains.**

It sustains vegetation.

It sustains water cycles.

It sustains microorganisms, fungi, fauna and roots.

It sustains the slow memory of the territory.

Without soil life, territorial continuity weakens.

---

## Operational question

**What sustains life?**

The Soil Tree responds to this question by observing the conditions that allow a territory to remain alive, fertile, resilient and capable of regeneration.

---

## Function within the open architecture

The Soil Tree is a basal reading organ.

Its reading is autonomous, but it acquires full meaning when it dialogues with the other organs.

Each tree contributes a reading.

BIOIA integrates the readings.

WUOM organizes the orientation.

The territory verifies the result through return.

---

## Reading layers of the soil

### Structure

Aggregation, porosity, compaction and texture.

### Organic matter

Quantity, quality, transformation and availability.

### Soil life

Microorganisms, fungi, fauna, roots and biological activity.

### Water and infiltration

Retention, movement, availability and loss.

### Nutrients and balance

Cycles, availability, exchange and dependency.

### Cover

Vegetation, mulch, residues and protection from erosion.

### Degradation signs

Erosion, compaction, loss of fertility, chemical dependence and biological simplification.

---

## Observable signals

The Soil Tree can register:

- humidity;
- soil cover;
- erosion;
- compaction;
- organic matter;
- root presence;
- fungi;
- earthworms and soil fauna;
- infiltration capacity;
- crusting;
- smell;
- color;
- temperature;
- vegetation response;
- recovery after rain.

These signals must always be interpreted together with water, territory, climate, biodiversity and management history.

## Ecological principle

A productive soil is not necessarily an autonomous soil.

A system can produce a lot while depending increasingly on external inputs to sustain that production.

For BIOIA / WUOM, productivity must be read together with autonomy, biodiversity, water retention and long-term continuity.

---

## Time as ecological cost

Soil degradation can happen quickly.

Regeneration usually requires years of continuity.

The time required to recover soil relationships is part of the real cost of degradation.

Time is also an ecological cost.

---

## Territorial return

Every soil reading must return to the territory.

Return may include:

- direct observation;
- seasonal comparison;
- soil cover monitoring;
- infiltration observation;
- vegetation response;
- biodiversity signals;
- comparison between managed plots;
- long-term regeneration records.

Without return, the reading remains incomplete.

---

## Orientation criteria

The Soil Tree can support decisions related to:

- soil cover;
- erosion prevention;
- water retention;
- organic matter recovery;
- reduction of external dependency;
- regenerative agriculture;
- climate adaptation;
- biodiversity support;
- minimum intervention.

Its objective is not to maximize control.

Its objective is to understand how the soil sustains life.
