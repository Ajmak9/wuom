# 02_TERRITORY_TREE_v1.0

## BIOIA / WUOM  
### Basal Family v1.0

---

## Purpose

The Territory Tree organizes the reading of territory as a living system of relationships within BIOIA / WUOM.

Its function is not to represent territory as a static surface, a map or a container.

It reads how soil, water, biodiversity, human activity, infrastructure, climate and governance interact in a situated place.

The territory is not a background.

It is a living system of relationships.

---

## Core principle

**The territory organizes.**

It organizes flows.

It organizes limits.

It organizes uses.

It organizes tensions.

It organizes coexistence between life, infrastructure, people and risk.

Territory is where relationships become situated.

---

## Operational question

**How are relationships organized?**

The Territory Tree responds to this question by observing how the different layers of a place interact, overlap and transform each other.

---

## Function within the open architecture

The Territory Tree is a relational reading organ.

Its reading is autonomous, but it acquires full meaning when it dialogues with the other organs.

Each tree contributes a reading.

BIOIA integrates the readings.

WUOM organizes the orientation.

The territory verifies the result through return.

---

## Reading layers of the territory

### Relief and structure

Mountains, slopes, plains, valleys, orientation and exposure.

### Water system

Rivers, streams, ravines, aquifers, wetlands, retention and flows.

### Land cover and use

Forests, agriculture, urban areas, artificial surfaces and natural spaces.

### Biodiversity

Habitats, species, ecological connectivity and ecological quality.

### Human footprint

Infrastructure, extraction, transformation, accessibility and pressure.

### Climate and exposure

Sun, wind, temperature, extreme events, vulnerability and resilience.

### Social and cultural layer

Communities, knowledge, practices, values, conflicts and agreements.

### Governance

Norms, protection figures, planning, participation and responsibility.

## Observable signals

The Territory Tree can register:

- landscape patterns;
- water presence and flow;
- vegetation condition;
- habitat connectivity;
- land use dynamics;
- fragmentation;
- erosion;
- pressure points;
- access routes;
- infrastructure impacts;
- climate exposure;
- community activity;
- cultural landscape;
- governance conflicts;

These signals must always be interpreted together with soil, water, climate, biodiversity and local experience.

### Drone Tree

The drone observes spatial patterns from above.

### Temperature Tree

The bee reveals ecological connectivity through movement, flowering and relationships.

### Eagle Tree

---

## Territorial principle

A territory is not the sum of its elements.

It is the pattern of relationships between them.

Elements describe.

Relationships sustain.

The territory is where those relationships become visible, conflictive, fragile or regenerative.

---

## Governance principle

A territory cannot be understood only from outside.

External data may help.

Remote sensing may help.

Models may help.

But territorial understanding requires situated verification.

Without return, territorial reading remains incomplete.

---

## Return and orientation

Every territorial reading must return to the place it describes.

Return may include:

- direct observation;
- field verification;
- comparison between seasons;
- dialogue with local actors;
- review of management practices;
- observation of impacts;
- monitoring of regeneration;
- correction of assumptions.

Return verifies learning and keeps orientation connected to reality.

---

## Orientation criteria

The Territory Tree can support decisions related to:

- land use;
- ecological connectivity;
- restoration;
- climate adaptation;
- infrastructure planning;
- biodiversity protection;
- water management;
- fire risk prevention;
- governance and participation;
- minimum intervention.

Its objective is not to control the territory.

Its objective is to understand how relationships are organized so decisions can be better oriented.
