# 03_DRONE_TREE_v1.0

## BIOIA / WUOM  
### Basal Family v1.0

---

## Purpose

The Drone Tree organizes the aerial reading of territory as a technical observation organ within BIOIA / WUOM.

Its function is not to replace human experience, field knowledge or territorial verification.

It expands observation when access is difficult, dangerous or too slow.

The drone extends the system’s view without replacing it.

---

## Core principle

**The drone observes.**

It does not decide.

It does not interpret by itself.

It does not replace situated knowledge.

It provides new perspectives that help understand the territory before action.

---

## Operational question

**What can we observe with lower risk?**

The Drone Tree responds to this question by expanding spatial understanding through aerial observation and systematic recording.

---

## Function within the open architecture

The Drone Tree is a technical reading organ.

Its reading is autonomous, but it acquires full meaning when it dialogues with the other organs.

Each tree contributes a reading.

BIOIA integrates the readings.

WUOM organizes the orientation.

The territory verifies the result through return.

---

## Reading layers of the drone

### Aerial view

Global understanding of landscape forms and spatial relationships.

### Mapping

Generation and updating of territorial information.

### Vegetation cover

Changes in vegetation, continuity, fragmentation and stress.

### Hydrology

Water courses, runoff, wetlands and visible surface dynamics.

### Risks

Fire, erosion, flooding, landslides, accessibility and exposure.

### Infrastructure

Paths, roads, lines, buildings, constructions and territorial elements.

### Temporal monitoring

Comparison of changes over time through repeated observations.

---

## Observable signals

The Drone Tree can register:

- land cover changes;
- degraded areas;
- hot spots;
- landscape fragmentation;
- ecological connectivity;
- restoration progress;
- fire damage;
- crop condition;
- access routes;
- hydrological changes;
- surface humidity patterns;
- regeneration processes;
- infrastructure pressure;
- inaccessible or risky areas.

These observations must always be contextualized through situated knowledge and territorial return.

---

## Relationship with other organs

### Soil Tree

The drone observes patterns.

The soil confirms processes.

### Territory Tree

The drone reveals spatial relationships.

The territory provides context.

### Water Tree

Water dynamics can be followed from the air through channels, wetlands, runoff and flooded areas.

### Temperature Tree

Thermal information may complement visual observation when appropriate.

### Bee Tree

The bee reveals ecological connectivity that the drone can only partially observe.

### Eagle Tree

The eagle inspires a high territorial reading.

The drone does not imitate nature.

It uses an aerial perspective to expand observation.

---

## Ethical principle

The drone is not a surveillance system.

It does not replace people.

It does not replace fieldwork.

Its function is to reduce uncertainty and improve territorial understanding.

The drone does not increase control.

It improves reading.

---

## Return and verification

Every aerial observation must return to the territory.

Return may include:

- field visits;
- temporal comparison;
- validation with local actors;
- comparison with environmental data;
- photographic documentation;
- assessment of interventions;
- correction of assumptions;
- review of observed patterns.

Without return, aerial observation remains incomplete.

---

## Orientation criteria

The Drone Tree can support decisions related to:

- fire prevention;
- ecological restoration;
- environmental monitoring;
- conservation;
- forest management;
- agriculture;
- damage assessment;
- territorial planning;
- minimum intervention;
- risk reduction.

Its objective is not to replace territorial knowledge.

Its objective is to improve the reading of the territory.
