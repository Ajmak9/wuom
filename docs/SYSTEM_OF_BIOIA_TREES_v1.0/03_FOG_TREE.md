# 03 · Fog Tree
## Uncertainty · Ambiguity · Low visibility · Containment

### Status
BIOIA / WUOM base document  
Operational tree of containment and uncertainty  
Version: v0.1

---

## 1. Definition

The Fog Tree is the tree that protects what cannot yet be seen clearly.

It does not eliminate uncertainty.

It does not force answers.

It does not accelerate decisions.

It recognizes that there are moments when understanding requires waiting.

Fog is not absence of information.

It is a temporary state of knowledge.

---

## 2. Function

The Fog Tree allows us to read:

- uncertainty
- ambiguity
- incomplete information
- low visibility
- processes in gestation
- weak signals
- apparent contradictions
- limits of knowledge

Its main function is to contain before precipitating a conclusion.

---

## 3. Operational principle

> Not every lack of clarity is a problem.  
> Sometimes it is a necessary condition for better understanding.

The Fog Tree organizes this reading:

**weak signal → uncertainty → observation → clarification → orientation**

---

## 4. Guiding questions

- What can we not yet understand?
- What information is missing?
- What remains ambiguous?
- What requires more time?
- What needs protection?
- What decision should be delayed?
- What would be irresponsible to affirm now?

---

## 5. Signals observed

The Fog Tree observes:

- incomplete information
- contradictory data
- weak signals
- emerging processes
- undefined risks
- changes that are difficult to interpret
- grey zones
- knowledge gaps
- territorial ambiguity
- low operational visibility

---

## 6. Fog as operational space

Fog does not block reading.

It slows it down.

It forces reduced speed, deeper observation and avoidance of premature conclusions.

Not every question needs an immediate answer.

Some need more territory, more time and more evidence.

---

## 7. BIOIA applications

The Fog Tree can be applied to:

- scientific research
- territorial governance
- risk management
- biodiversity
- environmental assessment
- citizen science
- complex social processes
- public decision-making
- conflict management
- adaptive systems

---

## 8. Ethics and custody

The Fog Tree protects what is not yet ready to become a public statement.

Principles:

- do not force artificial clarity
- do not publish hypotheses as facts
- do not turn uncertainty into fear
- do not decide without sufficient evidence
- respect the times of the territory
- safeguard sensitive information

---

## 9. Relation with other trees

### With the Horizon Tree

The Horizon opens scenarios.  
The Fog reminds us that some cannot yet be fully seen.

### With the Wind Tree

The Wind detects moving signals.  
The Fog protects those whose direction is not yet clear.

### With the Fire Tree

The Fire transforms.  
The Fog prevents transforming too early.

### With the Living Tree

The Living Tree maintains balance.  
The Fog protects the time needed for that balance to mature.

---

## 10. Expected result

The Fog Tree produces:

- more prudent decisions
- protection of sensitive information
- reduction of errors caused by precipitation
- better understanding before intervention
- responsible management of uncertainty
- orientation based on sufficient evidence

---

## 11. Short formula

> Fog is not the end of the path.  
> It is the space where the path is still forming.

---

## 12. Closing

The Fog Tree does not seek to eliminate uncertainty.

It learns to live with it until the territory allows greater clarity.

In BIOIA, containment is also a form of action.

Waiting can be a responsible decision when it protects the territory, people and knowledge.

**Without custody, there is no publication.**  
**Without return, there is no orientation.**
