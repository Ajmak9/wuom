### Status

---

## 1. Definition

The Time Tree organizes the rhythms of a living system.

It does not only measure hours, days or years.

It reads the different temporal scales that coexist within a territory and helps understand when a process needs acceleration, continuity or waiting.

Each phenomenon has its own time.

Understanding those rhythms is essential for good orientation.

---

## 2. Function

The Time Tree allows us to read:

- Ecological rhythms
- Temporal scales
- Process maturation
- Synchronies
- Cycles
- Speeds of change
- Windows of opportunity
- Permanence

Its main function is to orient decisions while respecting the specific time of each process.

---

## 3. Operational principle

> Not all processes evolve at the same rhythm.

The Time Tree organizes the reading:

**observation → rhythm → synchrony → decision → return → new cycle**

---

## 4. Guiding questions

- What is the natural rhythm of this process?
- What needs more time?
- What is happening too fast?
- What is being delayed?
- When is the right moment to intervene?
- Which cycles are repeating?
- Which temporal windows are opening?

The Time Tree observes:

## 6. Temporal scales

BIOIA recognizes multiple coexisting timescales.

Examples:

- Seconds — digital processing
- Minutes — events
- Hours — weather
- Days — biological response
- Seasons — ecosystems
- Years — territorial management
- Decades — forests, aquifers, restoration
- Generations — landscape and culture

No scale replaces another.

All scales must remain in dialogue.

## 8. Ethics and stewardship

Time must also be protected.

Principles:

- Do not accelerate living processes unnecessarily.
- Do not delay urgent decisions.
- Respect ecological rhythms.
- Do not use impatience as a technical criterion.
- Verify before concluding.
- Allow knowledge to mature.

The Time Tree reminds us that speed is not always progress.

Others require seasons.

Others require generations.
