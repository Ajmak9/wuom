# 01 · Horizon Tree
## Futures · Projections · Scenarios · Orientation

### Status
BIOIA / WUOM base document  
Operational tree of future-oriented reading  
Version: v0.1

---

## 1. Definition

The Horizon Tree is the tree that looks ahead.

It does not predict the future.  
It does not promise certainty.  
It does not decide by itself.

It opens the field of vision to explore possible futures, scenarios, risks, opportunities and directions of change.

---

## 2. Function

The Horizon Tree allows us to read:

- possible futures
- emerging scenarios
- projections
- opportunities
- risks
- trends
- visions
- decision pathways

Its main function is to widen the field of view before acting.

---

## 3. Operational principle

> Good orientation is impossible when only the present is observed.

The present contains traces of the past and openings toward the future.

The Horizon Tree organizes this reading:

**past read → present understood → possible futures → orientation**

---

## 4. Guiding questions

- What futures are possible?
- What signals announce them?
- What risks may emerge?
- What opportunities appear?
- What present decisions open or close pathways?
- What scenario should be avoided?
- What scenario requires preparation?

---

## 5. Signals observed

The Horizon Tree observes:

- slow trends
- accumulated changes
- territorial projections
- climate scenarios
- future risks
- available capacities
- current decisions
- historical signals
- change thresholds
- possible bifurcation points

---

## 6. Futures and projections

A projection is not a prophecy.

It is a hypothesis oriented by current signals and territorial memory.

The Horizon Tree does not say: “this will happen.”  
It says:

**if these signals continue, these pathways may open.**

---

## 7. BIOIA applications

The Horizon Tree can be applied to:

- territorial planning
- environmental scenarios
- public governance
- BIOIA / WUOM strategy
- climate risks
- minimal intervention design
- agricultural decisions
- future projects
- capacity reading
- institutional orientation

---

## 8. Ethics and custody

The Horizon Tree must not be used to manufacture fear or sell certainty.

Its reading requires prudence.

Principles:

- do not confuse scenario with destiny
- do not turn hypotheses into facts
- do not use the future as a threat
- do not hide uncertainty
- do not decide without territorial return
- do not project without memory

---

## 9. Relation with other trees

### With the Wind Tree

### With the Living Tree

---

## 10. Expected result

The Horizon Tree produces:

- future maps
- comparable scenarios
- risk readings
- opportunity identification
- strategic orientation
- preparation without precipitation

---

## 11. Short formula

> Looking at the horizon is not escaping the present; it is preparing it better.

---

## 12. Closing

The Horizon Tree does not guess.

It observes signals, opens scenarios and organizes possibilities.

It does not replace decision-making.

It prepares it.

**Good projects do not only solve present problems; they also build capacities to inhabit the future better.**
