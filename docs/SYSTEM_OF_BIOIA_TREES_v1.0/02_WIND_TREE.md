## Dispersion · Propagation · Signals · Zero movement

### Status

---

## 1. Definition

The Wind Tree is the tree that listens, disperses and carries signals.

It does not act through direct force.  
It does not push the territory.  
It does not impose direction.

It reads how signals move while they still seem small, weak or invisible.

Its seeds are data, intuitions, learnings, rumours, displacements, changes in perception and minimal movements.

---

## 2. Function

The Wind Tree allows us to read:

- signal dispersion
- information propagation
- subtle movement
- invisible changes
- emerging trends
- spread of ideas
- directions of expansion
- speed of change

Its main function is not to decide, but to detect where something is travelling.

---

## 3. Operational principle

> The wind is not seen, but it transforms.

A signal may seem isolated, but if it repeats, travels or changes scale, it is no longer mere noise.

The Wind Tree observes that transition:

**signal → repetition → dispersion → pattern → orientation**

---

## 4. Guiding questions

- What signals are dispersing?
- Where are they travelling?
- Who or what is carrying them?
- What movements seem invisible?
- What information spreads without central control?
- What changes even before it looks like change?
- Where do ideas, practices or alerts begin to propagate?

---

## 5. Signals observed

The Wind Tree observes:

- data starting to circulate
- narratives being repeated
- changes in language
- learning being transmitted
- territorial rumours
- emerging trends
- social movements
- shifts in attention
- changes in perception
- repeated small decisions

---

## 6. Zero movement

Zero movement does not mean absence of movement.

It means movement not yet visible.

It may be occurring in:

- the soil
- relationships
- public attention
- risk perception
- territorial memory
- the accumulation of weak signals

The Wind Tree detects what does not yet have stable form.

---

## 7. BIOIA applications

The Wind Tree can be applied to:

- ecosystems and biodiversity
- territorial governance
- citizen science
- public communication
- education and learning
- conflict reading
- propagation of environmental signals
- detection of cultural change
- narrative analysis
- strategic orientation

---

## 8. Ethics and custody

The Wind Tree must not be used to manipulate dispersion.

Its reading requires custody.

The purpose is not to control signals, but to understand how they circulate without breaking territorial balance.

Principles:

- do not manipulate propagation
- do not force narratives
- do not turn weak signals into alarm
- do not publish without context
- do not extract information without return
- do not confuse speed with truth

---

## 9. Relation with other trees

### With the Horizon Tree

The Horizon sees what may become possible.  
The Wind observes what is beginning to move.

### With the Fog Tree

The Fog contains uncertainty.  
The Wind detects what moves within that uncertainty.

### With the Fire Tree

The Fire transforms.  
The Wind may accelerate, disperse or feed that transformation.

### With the Living Tree

The Living Tree sustains the cycle.  
The Wind connects, distributes and returns signals to the system.

---

## 10. Expected result

The Wind Tree produces:

- dispersion maps
- readings of mobile signals
- understanding of subtle dynamics
- early detection of patterns
- orientation without premature intervention

---

## 11. Short formula

> Reading the wind means reading what moves before it becomes visible.

---

## 12. Closing

The Wind Tree does not control movement.

It listens to it.

It does not capture signals.

It follows them.

It does not turn all dispersion into truth.

It observes until the territory confirms whether there is pattern, noise or return.

**Without custody, there is no publication.**  
**Without return, there is no orientation.**
