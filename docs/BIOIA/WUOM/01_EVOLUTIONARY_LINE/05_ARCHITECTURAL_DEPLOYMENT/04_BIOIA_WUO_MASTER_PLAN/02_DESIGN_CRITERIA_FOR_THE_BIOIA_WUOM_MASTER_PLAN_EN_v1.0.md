# 02 · Design Criteria for the BIOIA / WUOM Master Plan

## Status

**Constitutive document**  
**Version:** Stable

---

## Purpose

This document defines the criteria that any representation of the BIOIA / WUOM Master Plan must meet.

It does not describe a specific aesthetic.

It describes the architectural conditions that allow the Master Plan to function as a stable reference for the system.

---

## 1. Permanence

The Master Plan represents the organisation of the system, not the state of a specific version.

It must remain valid even when the following evolve:

- Trees;
- Interfaces;
- Applications;
- languages;
- visual styles.

The structure is stable.

The contents are dynamic.

---

## 2. Neutrality

The Master Plan must be representable through different graphic languages without losing its identity.

It must function in:

- colour;
- black and white;
- greyscale;
- blueprint;
- technical diagram;
- hand drawing.

Structure takes priority over aesthetics.

Aesthetics must never be necessary in order to understand the plan.

---

## 3. Scalability

The Master Plan must accommodate growth without altering its fundamental architecture.

The system can expand through:

- new Trees;
- new Interfaces;
- new Applications;
- new operative layers.

Growth occurs through integration, not replacement.

The architecture remains.

---

## 4. Orientation

The primary function of the Master Plan is to situate each element within the whole.

It must visually answer one question:

> **Where is this piece located within BIOIA / WUOM?**

It does not explain.  
It does not detail.  
It does not narrate.

It orients.

---

## 5. Multilevel legibility

The Master Plan must support three levels of reading.

### Immediate reading

Recognise the overall organism:

- Core;
- Territory;
- Experience;
- AI-Reading;
- Return.

### Structural reading

Understand the architecture:

- Law;
- Cognitive Architecture;
- Principles;
- Tree Family;
- Interfaces.

### Detailed reading

Locate components and relationships:

- Applications;
- Technology;
- Ethics;
- Publication;
- State;
- Traceability.

Each level must remain visible without interfering with the others.

---

## 6. Systemic coherence

Every represented element must maintain an explicit relationship with the rest of the system.

There must be no:

- isolated components;
- modules without return;
- pieces without territory.

The Master Plan represents an organism, not a catalogue.

---

## 7. Technological independence

The Master Plan does not depend on:

- a tool;
- a platform;
- an implementation language;
- or a specific interface.

It represents a conceptual organisation.

Not a technical solution.

---

## 8. Centrality of the territory

Every flow begins and ends in the territory through territorial return.

The territory is not one module among others.

It is the structural condition that gives meaning to the system.

Its position in the Master Plan must reflect that centrality.

---

## 9. Subordinate AI

Artificial Intelligence appears as a reading and support capacity.

Never as the origin of the system or its purpose.

Its position in the Master Plan must express:

- functional subordination;
- dependence on the territory;
- coupling without collapse;
- service to orientation.

---

## 10. Canonical identity

The Master Plan constitutes the stable visual reference of BIOIA / WUOM.

The following may evolve:

- infographics;
- Trees;
- Interfaces;
- Applications;
- cases.

The Master Plan remains the canonical representation of the architecture.

---

## 11. Traceability

Every element of the Master Plan must be traceable to a real system document.

Examples:

- Law → Law of Territorial Return;
- Architecture → Cognitive Architecture;
- Principles → Foundational documents;
- Trees → Tree Family;
- Interfaces → Interface documents;
- Applications → Situated cases.

This turns the Master Plan into an architectural index, not a decorative image.

---

## 12. Structural economy

The Master Plan must represent the highest level of organisation with the minimum number of necessary elements.

If a component can be removed without altering understanding of the system, it does not belong in the Master Plan.

This criterion prevents the plan from becoming an inventory.

It preserves it as an essential structure.

---

## Closing statement

The Master Plan must be stable, legible, traceable, and scalable.

Its value does not lie in showing more.

It lies in showing what is necessary for BIOIA / WUOM to be understood, expanded, and maintained without losing coherence.

> **Without custody, there is no publication.**  
> **Without return, there is no orientation.**
