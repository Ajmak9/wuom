# 01 · ECOSYSTEMIC GOVERNANCE MODEL (EGM)

**Operational protocol for the custody of the evolution of the BIOIA / WUOM ecosystem**

**Version:** v1.0  
**Status:** Stable  
**Location:** `05_METAARCHITECTURE/01_ECOSYSTEMIC_GOVERNANCE_MODEL_EN.md`

---

## 0. General Principle

The Ecosystemic Governance Model (EGM) does not determine what must emerge.

It determines how to hold in custody, evaluate, orient and locate what emerges while preserving the coherence of the BIOIA / WUOM ecosystem.

Its function is not to create content.

Its function is to orient decisions.

---

## 1. Purpose

The EGM exists to orient decisions concerning any emerging component of the BIOIA / WUOM ecosystem.

Its operational question is:

> A new component has appeared. What is its place within the ecosystem?

The EGM does not define content.

It defines decision criteria.

Its objective is to reduce uncertainty as the ecosystem evolves while preserving coherence among ontology, methodology, architecture, applications and future extensions.

---

## 2. Ecosystemic Governance Cycle

Every emerging component moves through a maturation process before being integrated into the ecosystem.

```text
OBSERVATION
    ↓
DEVELOPMENT
    ↓
ARCHITECTURE
    ↓
CASES
    ↓
VALIDATION
    ↓
UTILITY
    ↓
MATURATION
    ↓
FUNCTION
    ↓
CHANNEL
    ↓
RETURN
    ↓
EVOLUTION
```

### Description of the phases

#### Observation

An idea, signal, need or intuition appears.

#### Development

Initial explorations, notes, prototypes or experiments are carried out.

#### Architecture

The component acquires structure, language, internal coherence and identity.

#### Cases

The component is applied to real or simulated situations.

#### Validation

Its utility, consistency and repeatability are tested.

#### Utility

It is confirmed that the component provides value within a given context.

#### Maturation

Stability, independence and function are evaluated.

#### Function

The role performed by the component within the ecosystem is recognized.

#### Channel

The place where the component provides the greatest value is determined.

#### Return

The experience obtained returns to the BIOIA / WUOM core.

#### Evolution

The ecosystem incorporates the learning and continues to develop.

---

## 3. Possible Functions of a Mature Component

A mature component may perform different functions within the ecosystem.

These include:

- knowledge;
- method;
- reference case;
- architecture;
- interface;
- training;
- research;
- product, only where appropriate.

The EGM does not assign functions.

It recognizes them when a component reaches sufficient maturity.

---

## 4. Maturation Criteria

A component may be considered mature when it sufficiently meets the following criteria.

### Coherence

It is compatible with the ontology, principles and architecture of the ecosystem.

### Utility

It provides real value or addresses a concrete problem.

### Repeatability

It demonstrates consistency in more than one case.

### Documentation

It can be explained, understood and transmitted.

### Independence

It can be used without requiring a complete understanding of the ecosystem core.

### Return

It produces learning that strengthens BIOIA / WUOM.

---

## 5. Channel Criterion

The channel does not define the destination.

The function defines the channel.

The channel is a consequence, not an objective.

Depending on the nature of the component, the appropriate channel may be:

- GitHub;
- Notion;
- LinkedIn;
- Gumroad;
- a scientific or public-facing article;
- a workshop or training programme;
- collaboration;
- research;
- other spaces compatible with its function.

The objective is never to occupy a channel.

The objective is to find the place where the component provides the greatest value without losing coherence with the ecosystem.

---

## 6. Custody Criteria

Governance protects the coherence of the ecosystem through internal rules of evolution.

These include:

- maintain a stable core;
- do not reopen closed versions without sufficient justification;
- develop experimental branches outside the core;
- BIOIA does not constitute a commercial product;
- interfaces may be distributed when they reach an appropriate level of maturity;
- utility does not imply commercialization;
- validation precedes independence;
- function precedes channel;
- return is an obligatory part of the evolutionary process.

### Fundamental rule

> A component modifies the core only when there is sufficient evidence that it improves coherence without unnecessarily increasing complexity.

This rule protects ecosystem stability and prevents growth through mere accumulation.

---

## 7. Final Principle

The maturity of a component is not measured by its visibility.

It is measured by the clarity of the function it performs within the ecosystem.

Governance consists of helping each component find that place without compromising the coherence of the whole.

---

## 8. Scope

The Ecosystemic Governance Model does not replace:

- ontology;
- principles;
- methodology;
- architecture;
- applications;
- BIOIA / WUOM cases.

It acts as a metaarchitectural layer designed to orient the evolution of the ecosystem and preserve its coherence as new components appear.

---

## 9. Revision of the EGM

The Ecosystemic Governance Model is itself part of the ecosystem.

It may be revised when accumulated experience demonstrates that one of its criteria no longer adequately orients the evolution of the whole.

Every modification must preserve its original purpose:

> Hold the coherence of the ecosystem in custody without limiting its capacity to evolve.
