# 07 · TWO POSSIBLE ORDERS: WHO CONTROLS AND FOR WHAT PURPOSE

**BIOIA / WUOM**  
**Directory:** `05_METAARCHITECTURE`  
**Status:** v0.1  
**Nature:** metaarchitecture document  
**Scope:** artificial intelligence, power, governance, territory, institutions, economy and return

---

## 1. Purpose

This document expands the reading of the two possible orders through a central question:

> Who controls artificial intelligence, what do they control and for what purpose does it operate?

The problem is not only to determine whether artificial intelligence is regulated or supervised.

The decisive issue is to identify:

- who defines the purpose;
- who owns the infrastructure;
- who controls the data;
- who establishes the criteria;
- who can gain access;
- who deliberates;
- who decides;
- who receives the benefits;
- who absorbs the costs;
- what capacity returns to the territory.

---

## 2. Central Thesis

Controlling artificial intelligence is not enough.

If the network continues to define the purpose of the system, concentrate infrastructure, restrict access and transfer costs to the territory, the order remains inverted.

The change of order begins when:

- the planet establishes conditions and limits;
- the territory verifies consequences;
- people and institutions retain deliberation;
- artificial intelligence functions as a distributed tool;
- the network connects without replacing;
- return strengthens common capacities.

---

## 3. The Incomplete Question

The question:

> Who controls artificial intelligence?

is necessary but insufficient.

Technical, legal, corporate, state or military control may exist without an orientation compatible with the continuity of life.

The complete question is:

> Who controls which capacity, for what purpose, through which infrastructure, under what limits, with what responsibility and with what return?

---

## 4. First Order: Concentrated Control

### 4.1. Definition

In the inverted order, a small number of actors control the decisive elements of the technological architecture:

- infrastructure;
- data centres;
- models;
- platforms;
- access;
- financing;
- design;
- criteria;
- intellectual property;
- decision-making capacity.

Artificial intelligence does not appear as a common capacity but as concentrated infrastructure that organizes relations, markets, information and behaviour from a purpose defined by a limited number of actors.

### 4.2. Actors of Control

Control may be concentrated in:

- large technology companies;
- infrastructure owners;
- investment funds;
- states;
- military bodies;
- digital platforms;
- cloud providers;
- model owners;
- data intermediaries;
- financial markets.

### 4.3. Objects of Control

These actors may control:

- artificial intelligence models;
- data centres;
- databases;
- platforms;
- recommendation systems;
- surveillance algorithms;
- satellites;
- connections;
- intellectual property;
- interfaces;
- standards;
- distribution channels;
- access to computing capacity.

### 4.4. Dominant Purposes

When the system is inverted, artificial intelligence may be directed primarily towards:

- accumulation;
- profitability;
- market expansion;
- surveillance;
- classification;
- data extraction;
- access control;
- acceleration;
- reduction of labour costs;
- geopolitical advantage;
- military superiority;
- technological dependence.

---

## 5. Upward Flow Towards the Network

The inverted order requires material, cognitive and social resources that flow upward from the territory towards the network.

These include:

- data;
- attention;
- labour;
- knowledge;
- energy;
- water;
- minerals;
- creativity;
- experience;
- time;
- economic resources;
- biodiversity;
- public infrastructure;
- social stability.

These resources feed an architecture that may grow without returning equivalent capacities to the territory on which it depends.

---

## 6. Downward Consequences

As resources flow upward, costs flow downward.

The consequences include:

- dependence;
- inequality;
- loss of autonomy;
- surveillance;
- precarization;
- energy consumption;
- water extraction;
- environmental degradation;
- opacity;
- replacement of capacities;
- concentration of power;
- institutional weakening;
- collective cost.

### 6.1. Sequence of the Inverted Order

**The network defines the purpose → artificial intelligence optimizes → the territory absorbs the cost**

---

## 7. The Limit of Regulation

Regulation can:

- establish obligations;
- reduce certain risks;
- impose transparency;
- require audits;
- protect rights;
- limit certain uses.

But regulation does not necessarily change the order when:

- ownership remains concentrated;
- the purpose continues to be defined by profitability;
- access remains restricted;
- infrastructure remains outside public control;
- territorial costs continue to be externalized;
- collective deliberation does not participate in decisions.

Therefore:

> Controlling artificial intelligence without changing the purpose of the system does not alter the order.

---

## 8. Second Order: Distributed Capacity

### 8.1. Definition

In coexistence in continuity, artificial intelligence is organized as a distributed tool at the service of people, institutions, communities and territories.

It does not replace the collective capacity for orientation.

It expands it, relates it and returns capacities.

### 8.2. Basis of the System

The basis is formed by:

- territory;
- planetary conditions;
- ecological limits;
- communities;
- public institutions;
- public services;
- universities;
- diverse knowledge;
- situated experience;
- deliberation;
- responsibility.

### 8.3. Fields of Application

The network and artificial intelligence may support:

- health;
- well-being;
- education;
- agriculture;
- food;
- mobility;
- energy;
- water;
- biodiversity;
- culture;
- knowledge;
- public services;
- research;
- institutional coordination.

### 8.4. Operating Principles

- custody;
- limits;
- coherence;
- responsibility;
- care;
- transparency;
- auditability;
- proportional access;
- human decision-making;
- territorial return.

---

## 9. The Territory Does Not Decide by Itself

The planet and the territory do not replace human deliberation.

Their function within the architecture is to:

- establish material conditions;
- reveal limits;
- record impacts;
- verify consequences;
- reveal incompatibilities;
- verify return.

People and institutions retain the responsibility to decide.

The correct formulation is therefore:

> The planet establishes conditions and limits.  
> The territory verifies.  
> People and institutions deliberate and decide.

---

## 10. Role of Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence can:

- read signals;
- relate data;
- compare scenarios;
- show uncertainties;
- identify patterns;
- translate complexity;
- facilitate coordination;
- document impacts;
- verify returns;
- present possibilities.

It must not:

- define the purpose by itself;
- replace deliberation;
- decide which territory may be sacrificed;
- determine which loss is acceptable;
- convert life into a total score;
- displace human responsibility;
- close access to common knowledge.

---

## 11. Operational Cycle

Coexistence in continuity is organized through the following cycle:

1. **Territory**  
   Establishes conditions and limits.

2. **Signals**  
   Provides observations, data, changes and alerts.

3. **Experience**  
   Contextualizes through people and communities.

4. **Knowledge**  
   Integrates scientific, technical, cultural and situated forms of knowledge.

5. **Deliberation**  
   Contrasts options, conflicts and responsibilities.

6. **Responsible decision**  
   Remains with people and institutions.

7. **Artificial intelligence**  
   Helps read, relate and present possibilities.

8. **Return**  
   Returns capacities, services, benefits and knowledge to the territory.

---

## 12. Types of Control

### 12.1. Democratic Control

Seeks to:

- protect rights;
- distribute responsibility;
- enable auditing;
- maintain public capacity;
- guarantee deliberation;
- ensure access;
- prevent abusive concentration.

### 12.2. Corporate Control

Seeks to:

- protect ownership;
- expand markets;
- maintain advantage;
- control infrastructure;
- capture rents;
- restrict access.

### 12.3. State Control

May seek:

- security;
- administration;
- sovereignty;
- surveillance;
- planning;
- service provision;
- geopolitical power.

### 12.4. Military Control

Seeks:

- operational superiority;
- intelligence;
- surveillance;
- automation;
- response speed;
- strategic advantage.

### 12.5. Territorial Control

Seeks to:

- limit water consumption;
- limit energy consumption;
- protect soil;
- protect biodiversity;
- prevent cumulative impacts;
- maintain ecological continuity;
- verify returns.

### 12.6. Community Control

Seeks:

- autonomy;
- access;
- shared knowledge;
- local utility;
- decision-making capacity;
- common return;
- protection from extraction.

---

## 13. Control Is Not Equivalent to Orientation

A system may be controlled and continue to serve a destructive purpose.

There may be:

- technical control without democratic control;
- corporate control without territorial responsibility;
- state control without transparency;
- military control without civilian limits;
- legal control without return;
- environmental control without local decision-making capacity.

Orientation requires answers to the following questions:

- why does the system exist;
- which limits must it not exceed;
- who retains the decision;
- how are benefits distributed;
- where do costs fall;
- what does it return to the territory.

---

## 14. Artificial Intelligence as a Collective Capacity

Artificial intelligence depends on a basis that no company or institution has created by itself:

- language;
- culture;
- science;
- education;
- public research;
- technical labour;
- accumulated knowledge;
- infrastructure;
- energy;
- water;
- minerals;
- territories;
- human interaction.

Private legal ownership may exist over models, infrastructure or licences.

But their origin is collective and their impact is systemic.

Therefore:

> Artificial intelligence is a capacity of collective origin, systemic impact and common responsibility, even when it is under private ownership.

---

## 15. Territorial Return

Artificial intelligence must return verifiable capacities to the territory on which it depends.

Return may include:

- accessible knowledge;
- improved public services;
- literacy;
- open tools;
- institutional capacity;
- reduction of impacts;
- restoration;
- local autonomy;
- transparency;
- shared infrastructure;
- community strengthening;
- redistribution of benefits.

Return must not be subsequent compensation or a philanthropic action.

It must form part of the architecture from the beginning.

---

## 16. Who Receives the Benefit

The evaluation of a technological architecture must identify:

- who receives income;
- who gains capacity;
- who obtains knowledge;
- who controls the results;
- who has access to the tool;
- who can reuse it;
- who is excluded;
- who pays the material costs;
- who assumes the risks.

The existence of aggregate benefits does not demonstrate the existence of collective or territorial return.

---

## 17. Who Absorbs the Cost

Costs may fall on:

- communities near data centres;
- territories facing water scarcity;
- energy systems;
- workers;
- public services;
- ecosystems;
- authors and cultural producers;
- weakened institutions;
- classified or surveilled people;
- future generations.

A just architecture must prevent benefit from being concentrated while cost is distributed.

---

## 18. Orientation Criteria

Before adopting an artificial intelligence solution, the following questions must be answered:

- What problem is being addressed?
- Is artificial intelligence necessary?
- What technological scale is proportional?
- Who defines the purpose?
- Who controls the infrastructure?
- Who controls the data?
- Who can audit?
- Who decides?
- Who is accountable for harm?
- Where are water and energy consumed?
- Which territory receives the impact?
- What capacities are returned?
- Can the system be stopped?
- Can it be replaced by a simpler solution?
- Does the community retain autonomy?

---

## 19. Signals of Inverted Order

- a small number of actors control the infrastructure;
- access is restricted;
- the purpose is not deliberated;
- profitability precedes need;
- artificial intelligence replaces capacities;
- territorial costs remain hidden;
- regulation arrives after deployment;
- benefit is concentrated;
- return is absent;
- the territory functions as passive support.

---

## 20. Signals of Coexistence in Continuity

- the purpose is public;
- limits are defined;
- the scale is proportional;
- access can be justified;
- artificial intelligence supports;
- people and institutions decide;
- infrastructure is auditable;
- costs are visible;
- the territory verifies;
- return is concrete;
- responsibility is not delegated;
- capacities are distributed.

---

## 21. Synthesis Formulas

### Inverted order

**The network defines the purpose → artificial intelligence optimizes → the territory absorbs the cost**

### Coexistence in continuity

**The planet establishes conditions and limits → the territory verifies → people and institutions decide → artificial intelligence helps read → the network connects → the territory receives return**

### Central question

**Who controls what, for what purpose and with what return?**

### Closing

**Artificial intelligence must not replace the collective capacity for orientation. It must expand it, distribute it and return capacities to the whole.**
