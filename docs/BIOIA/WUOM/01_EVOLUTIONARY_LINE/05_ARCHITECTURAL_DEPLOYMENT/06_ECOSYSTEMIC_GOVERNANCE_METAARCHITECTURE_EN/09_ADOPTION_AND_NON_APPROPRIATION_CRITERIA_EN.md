# 09 · ADOPTION AND NON-APPROPRIATION CRITERIA

**BIOIA / WUOM**  
**Directory:** `05_METAARCHITECTURE`  
**Status:** v0.1  
**Nature:** metaarchitecture and custody document  
**Scope:** adoption, adaptation, verification, use of the name, openness, traceability and territorial return

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes criteria for distinguishing among:

- inspiration;
- compatibility;
- adoption;
- implementation;
- adaptation;
- appropriation;
- cosmetic absorption;
- legitimate use of the BIOIA name.

BIOIA is a public and open architecture. Its openness allows other people, communities, institutions and territories to learn, use, adapt or discuss its principles.

That openness does not mean that any partial use of the vocabulary may be presented as a BIOIA implementation.

The purpose of this document is not to close access or create a central authority.

Its function is to protect the coherence of the operational grammar through public, verifiable and revisable criteria.

---

## 2. Problem

Any open architecture may be:

- understood;
- applied;
- adapted;
- transformed;
- ignored;
- reduced;
- instrumentalized;
- absorbed by structures that preserve the previous order.

The main risk is not only rejection.

The vocabulary may also be adopted superficially without modifying:

- who defines the purpose;
- who controls the infrastructure;
- who retains the decision;
- who receives the benefit;
- who absorbs the cost;
- what returns to the territory.

This situation is called:

## Cosmetic Absorption

Cosmetic absorption uses words such as:

- territory;
- care;
- sustainability;
- return;
- participation;
- responsible artificial intelligence;
- governance;
- community;

without changing the real architecture of the system.

---

## 3. Protective Principle

> BIOIA may be adapted, but it cannot be emptied of territory, deliberation, responsibility and return without ceasing to be BIOIA.

Openness allows the form to be transformed.

It does not allow the purpose to be inverted while the name is retained as though nothing had changed.

---

## 4. Non-Negotiable Core

A practice should not be presented as a BIOIA implementation unless it simultaneously meets the following conditions.

### 4.1. Territory

The territory must:

- be identified;
- provide signals;
- establish conditions and limits;
- appear as a material and living basis;
- participate in the verification of consequences.

The territory cannot be reduced to:

- a data source;
- a setting;
- a resource provider;
- a compensation surface;
- passive support.

### 4.2. Situated Experience

Experience must:

- contextualize signals;
- identify relations not visible in the data;
- incorporate local, technical, cultural and institutional knowledge;
- remain differentiated from artificial intelligence;
- participate in orientation.

### 4.3. Deliberation and Decision

People and institutions must retain:

- deliberation;
- decision-making;
- responsibility;
- the capacity to correct;
- the capacity to stop the system.

Artificial intelligence must not appear as the final authority.

### 4.4. Artificial Intelligence as Support

Artificial intelligence may:

- read;
- relate;
- compare;
- present possibilities;
- identify patterns;
- show uncertainty;
- help verify.

It must not:

- define the purpose by itself;
- replace deliberation;
- conceal responsibilities;
- automatically legitimize a decision;
- transform a loss into a mere score.

### 4.5. Visibility of Resources, Costs and Impacts

Every implementation must identify:

- resources used;
- water consumption;
- energy consumption;
- infrastructure;
- data;
- labour;
- risks;
- territorial impacts;
- benefited actors;
- affected actors.

### 4.6. Verifiable Territorial Return

A concrete capacity must be returned to the territory.

Return may include:

- knowledge;
- autonomy;
- infrastructure;
- services;
- restoration;
- learning;
- tools;
- institutional capacity;
- reduction of impacts;
- ecological continuity;
- community capacity.

Without verifiable return, there is no BIOIA closure.

---

## 5. Degrees of Relationship with BIOIA

Four degrees are distinguished to prevent confusion.

### 5.1. Inspired by BIOIA

A practice is inspired by BIOIA when it:

- uses some ideas;
- cites one or more principles;
- adopts part of the vocabulary;
- does not apply the complete grammar.

It must be presented as:

> Inspired by BIOIA.

It must not be presented as a BIOIA implementation.

### 5.2. Compatible with BIOIA

A practice is compatible with BIOIA when it:

- corresponds with several principles;
- maintains territorial orientation;
- respects deliberation;
- produces some return;
- was not designed or verified through the complete architecture.

It must be presented as:

> Compatible with BIOIA.

Compatibility does not imply formal adoption or belonging.

### 5.3. BIOIA Adaptation

A BIOIA adaptation:

- explicitly begins from the grammar;
- modifies elements in response to a specific territory;
- documents the changes;
- preserves the non-negotiable core;
- identifies responsibilities;
- demonstrates territorial return.

The adaptation must indicate:

- what is preserved;
- what is modified;
- why it is modified;
- who participates;
- how it is verified.

### 5.4. BIOIA Implementation

A BIOIA implementation must:

- apply the operational grammar;
- identify the territory;
- integrate situated experience;
- maintain human deliberation;
- define the role of artificial intelligence;
- document infrastructure and costs;
- establish responsible parties;
- demonstrate territorial return;
- permit auditing;
- permit correction or stopping.

---

## 6. Naming Rule

Public materials may be used in accordance with their licence.

Use of the expression:

> BIOIA implementation

requires evidence that the operational criteria have been met.

Use of the name must not depend on a unilateral declaration.

It must be accompanied by sufficient public evidence.

---

## 7. Minimum Evidence

An implementation should be able to document, at minimum:

1. territorial problem;
2. affected territory;
3. observed signals;
4. situated experience incorporated;
5. participating actors;
6. explicit purpose;
7. established limits;
8. infrastructure used;
9. data used;
10. role of artificial intelligence;
11. parties responsible for the decision;
12. material costs;
13. risks;
14. benefits;
15. territorial return;
16. verification mechanism;
17. possibility of correction;
18. possibility of stopping.

---

## 8. Test Against Cosmetic Absorption

An adoption must clearly answer five questions.

### Question 1

Did the party defining the purpose change?

### Question 2

Did the party retaining the decision change?

### Question 3

Did the party receiving the benefit change?

### Question 4

Did the party absorbing the cost change?

### Question 5

Is there verifiable territorial return?

If the answers continue to reproduce the previous order, there has been no change of order.

Only the language has changed.

---

## 9. Signals of Genuine Adoption

An adoption is coherent when:

- the purpose is public;
- the territory establishes limits;
- experience participates;
- the technological scale is proportional;
- artificial intelligence acts as support;
- responsibility remains identifiable;
- costs are not externalized;
- benefit is not concentrated without return;
- territorial verification exists;
- the practice can be stopped or corrected;
- documentation remains accessible.

---

## 10. Signals of Cosmetic Absorption

There is a risk of cosmetic absorption when:

- the vocabulary is used without changing the purpose;
- participation is presented without real decision-making capacity;
- return is discussed without being demonstrated;
- subsequent compensation is called sustainability;
- artificial intelligence is incorporated without its necessity being justified;
- concentration of infrastructure is preserved;
- water, energy or data costs are concealed;
- an internal audit is presented as sufficient verification;
- the territory is used as a data provider;
- BIOIA is used as a reputational brand;
- the community appears as an image but not as an actor;
- the system cannot be stopped;
- profitability continues to occupy the centre.

---

## 11. Incompatibilities

The following practices should not be presented as BIOIA:

- propaganda;
- manipulation;
- population profiling;
- indiscriminate surveillance;
- automation without an accountable party;
- data extraction without return;
- replacement of deliberation;
- total classification of people or territories;
- economic compensation for irreversible losses;
- appropriation of situated knowledge;
- centralization disguised as participation;
- technological expansion without proportionality;
- systematic externalization of costs;
- privatization of common return.

---

## 12. Non-Appropriation

### 12.1. Principle

BIOIA may recognize situated capacities that already exist.

It cannot appropriate them.

### 12.2. BIOIA Must Not

- attribute affiliation;
- incorporate people without consent;
- represent other practices as its own;
- speak on behalf of communities;
- absorb territorial knowledge;
- replace identities;
- convert relationships into brand expansion;
- use names or images without authorization.

### 12.3. Voluntary Relationship

A conscious relationship with BIOIA must be:

- voluntary;
- explicit;
- reciprocal;
- contextual;
- revisable;
- compatible with the autonomy of the node.

---

## 13. Situated Nodes

A situated node may exist without knowing BIOIA.

BIOIA may recognize:

- correspondence;
- compatibility;
- convergence;
- territorial capacity.

That recognition does not turn the node into part of an organization.

The correct formula is:

> BIOIA recognizes existing nodes and enables voluntary relationships.

Not:

> BIOIA incorporates nodes.

---

## 14. Public Traceability

The main protection of an open architecture is traceability.

The following must be preserved:

- date;
- version;
- authorship;
- licence;
- change history;
- source documents;
- associated principles;
- application cases;
- custody decisions;
- modifications;
- closures.

Traceability makes it possible to distinguish:

- origin;
- adaptation;
- transformation;
- appropriation;
- incompatibility.

---

## 15. Licence and Coherence

The licence permits certain uses of the content.

The licence does not guarantee coherence with the grammar.

A reuse may be legal and, at the same time, incompatible with BIOIA.

Two levels must therefore be distinguished:

### Legal level

Determines which uses are permitted by the licence.

### Operational level

Determines whether a practice complies with the BIOIA grammar.

---

## 16. Distributed Verification

BIOIA must not create a central governing body that grants legitimacy unilaterally.

Verification must be:

- public;
- criteria-based;
- reasoned;
- revisable;
- distributed;
- open to objections;
- linked to the affected territory.

Custody does not consist of monopolizing interpretation.

It consists of keeping the core visible and permitting review.

---

## 17. Responsibility

Every implementation must identify:

- who designs;
- who finances;
- who controls the infrastructure;
- who provides data;
- who interprets;
- who decides;
- who executes;
- who receives benefits;
- who assumes risks;
- who is accountable for harm;
- who verifies return.

Responsibility cannot disappear behind:

- artificial intelligence;
- the algorithm;
- the platform;
- the organization;
- technical complexity.

---

## 18. Technological Proportionality

The adoption of artificial intelligence must be justified.

Before deployment, it must be evaluated whether:

- the problem requires artificial intelligence;
- a simpler tool exists;
- the scale is appropriate;
- the cost is proportional;
- the infrastructure is necessary;
- the system can be maintained;
- the return compensates for the impact.

Technological power is not a sufficient criterion.

---

## 19. Territorial Return

Territorial return must be:

- concrete;
- verifiable;
- proportional;
- accessible;
- sustained;
- linked to the territory;
- distinct from reputational compensation.

It is not enough to:

- publish a report;
- donate part of the benefit;
- plant trees outside the affected territory;
- provide a tool without decision-making capacity;
- declare a positive impact.

Return must strengthen territorial continuity.

---

## 20. Review and Correction

Every implementation must permit:

- periodic review;
- objection;
- correction;
- suspension;
- withdrawal;
- updating;
- learning.

A practice that cannot be corrected is not coherent with an orientation architecture.

---

## 21. Synthesis Formulas

### Cosmetic absorption

**Adopts the words and preserves the order.**

### Genuine adoption

**Changes purpose, decision-making, costs and return.**

### Non-appropriation

**BIOIA recognizes existing capacities; it does not incorporate or represent them without consent.**

### Custody

**Openness is protected through traceability, public criteria and territorial verification.**

### Closing

**Without territory, deliberation, responsibility and return, there is no BIOIA implementation.**

---

## 22. Checklist

A practice may be presented as a BIOIA implementation when it can answer yes to the following questions:

- Is the territory identified?
- Are explicit limits established?
- Does situated experience participate?
- Is the purpose documented?
- Does deliberation remain active?
- Are the decision-makers accountable?
- Does artificial intelligence act as support?
- Is the scale proportional?
- Are the resources used visible?
- Are the costs identified?
- Is benefit distributed?
- Is there territorial return?
- Can the return be verified?
- Can the practice be corrected?
- Can the practice be stopped?
- Is the relationship with nodes voluntary?
- Is the documentation public?
- Is the licence respected?
