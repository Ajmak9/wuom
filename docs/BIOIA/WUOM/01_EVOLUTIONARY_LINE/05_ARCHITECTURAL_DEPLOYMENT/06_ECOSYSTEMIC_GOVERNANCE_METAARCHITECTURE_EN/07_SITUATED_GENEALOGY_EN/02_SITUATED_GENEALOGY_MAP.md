## 1. Purpose

This document develops the cartographic reading of the image **Situated Genealogy · Map**.

The image does not represent a conventional family tree or a linear succession of antecedents. It organizes a network of relationships that influence a situated experience and its capacity to act, understand, and orient.

Its function is to show that no experience appears in isolation.

```text
living experience
→ relationships
→ conditions
→ decisions
→ orientation
```

Genealogy is understood here as a map of provenance, influences, limits, resources, and transformations.

## 3. Center of the map: living experience

The core of the image is **living experience**.

It includes:

- memory;
- practice;
- emotions;
- decisions;
- perception;
- learning;
- body;
- temporal continuity.

Living experience is not a closed entity. It is formed and transformed through relationships.

```text
experience
≠ isolated interior

experience
= relationship among body, memory, environment, and action
```

It therefore occupies the center of the map: it receives influences, produces decisions, and returns effects to the territory and the community.

---

## 4. Identity and values

Identity is not presented as a fixed essence.

It is formed through:

- experiences;
- bonds;
- decisions;
- conflicts;
- learning;
- places;
- languages;
- responsibilities.

Values do not appear as an abstract list either. They are verified in practice.

```text
declared value
→ decision
→ effect
→ verification
```

In BIOIA / WUOM, identity and values orient the way of reading, stewarding, and returning.

---

## 5. Family and background

Family and background contribute:

- narratives;
- memories;
- learning;
- material inheritances;
- symbolic inheritances;
- conflicts;
- continuities;
- ruptures.

They do not completely determine the trajectory.

They function as a layer of provenance that can be recognized, reinterpreted, or transformed.

```text
inheritance
≠ destiny

inheritance
→ condition of reading
```

Situated genealogy does not idealize antecedents. It incorporates them as part of the map.

---

## 6. Education and reference points

Education includes both institutional forms and informal learning.

It contributes:

- knowledge;
- languages;
- techniques;
- interpretive frameworks;
- reference points;
- access possibilities;
- cultural limits.

Teachers and reference points are not only people.

They may also be:

- works;
- territories;
- animals;
- errors;
- crises;
- tools;
- observation processes.

```text
teaching
→ does not always arrive in the form of a lesson
```

---

## 7. Territory and place

The territory contributes material and relational conditions:

- soil;
- water;
- climate;
- terrain;
- vegetation;
- fauna;
- infrastructure;
- distances;
- rhythms;
- memory.

Place is not interchangeable.

```text
territory
≠ background

territory
→ agent
```

In situated genealogy, the territory participates in shaping the gaze and verifying decisions.

---

## 8. Community and culture

Community and culture contribute:

- language;
- customs;
- narratives;
- norms;
- belonging;
- support;
- conflict;
- collective memory;
- forms of cooperation.

Community is not presented as a homogeneous unit.

It may contain differences, tensions, and partial relationships.

```text
community
= bond
+ difference
+ negotiation
```

Culture gives meaning, but it can also limit what is considered visible, possible, or legitimate.

---

## 9. Nature and biodiversity

Nature and biodiversity do not form a layer external to human life.

They directly participate in:

- food;
- water;
- temperature;
- health;
- shelter;
- fertility;
- pollination;
- balance;
- learning;
- orientation.

Situated genealogy recognizes that we coexist with other forms of life and depend on them.

```text
human@
⊂ living territory
```

The relationship with biodiversity is neither decorative nor merely moral. It is structural.

---

## 10. Society and systems

Experience is also conditioned by broader structures:

- institutions;
- laws;
- economy;
- technology;
- market;
- networks;
- administration;
- services;
- media;
- infrastructure.

These structures can:

- expand capacities;
- distribute resources;
- impose limits;
- produce dependence;
- accelerate processes;
- fragment relationships;
- make impacts invisible.

```text
situated experience
↔ broader systems
```

Genealogy makes it possible to recognize these influences without reducing experience to them.

---

## 11. Resources and tools

Resources may be:

- material;
- economic;
- technical;
- cognitive;
- social;
- territorial;
- temporal;
- symbolic.

Tools expand capacity, but they also orient the way of acting.

```text
tool
→ possibility
+ bias
+ potential dependence
```

AI enters this layer as a relational and reading tool, but its use must remain subordinate to judgment, context, and return.

---

## 12. Challenges and crises

Crises can transform the trajectory.

They do not always produce automatic learning.

They may generate:

- rupture;
- exhaustion;
- loss;
- displacement;
- reorganization;
- attention;
- change of judgment;
- new capacities.

```text
crisis
≠ guaranteed learning
```

Learning appears when there is reading, time, relationship, and the possibility of return.

In BIOIA, crisis can act as a signal, but it must not become a permanent aesthetic of alarm.

---

## 13. Decisions and actions

Decisions connect what is received with what is done.

```text
provenance
→ interpretation
→ decision
→ action
→ consequence
```

Actions show how values, limits, and resources actually operate.

A situated decision considers:

- context;
- scale;
- time;
- affected beings;
- reversibility;
- uncertainty;
- return.

Genealogy does not seek to justify every decision through the past. It seeks to make visible the conditions from which decisions are made.

---

## 14. Purpose and vision

Purpose orients what one seeks to build, sustain, or leave behind.

It is not understood as a rigid goal separated from the present.

```text
purpose
→ direction
not
→ closed destination
```

Vision must remain open to:

- verification;
- correction;
- territorial changes;
- new signals;
- unforeseen limits;
- later learning.

In BIOIA / WUOM, the central purpose is not to expand the system, but to increase the capacity for orientation and return.

## 15. Reading the visual architecture

The image organizes relationships through a tree.

### Canopy

It represents relationships that are visible or close to identity:

- family;
- education;
- territory;
- community;
- nature;
- society.

### Trunk

It represents the continuity of living experience.

### Roots

They represent what sustains or transforms action:

- resources;
- decisions;
- crises;
- purpose.

The network of connections prevents a linear reading.

```text
no layer acts alone
```

Each relationship may influence several layers at the same time.

---

## 16. BIOIA operation

The map is interpreted through the sequence:

```text
observe
→ connect
→ understand
→ orient
→ return
```

### Observe

Recognize elements without reducing them prematurely.

### Connect

Relate influences, dependencies, and effects.

### Understand

Provide context to the signal and the experience.

### Orient

Choose through situated judgment.

### Return

Return capacity to the territory and to life.

This sequence turns the map into a reading tool rather than a static classification.


But each term contains internal layers.

### human@

- memory;
- identity;
- background;
- education;
- bonds;
- decisions;
- purpose.

### territory

Includes:

- nature;
- biodiversity;
- community;

```text
minimum formula
```

## 18. Autonomy and non-universalization

The map is not intended as a mandatory list for every node.

Each genealogy may incorporate other layers:

- migration;
- language;
- illness;
- occupation;
- conflict;
- spirituality;
- technology;
- loss;
- cooperation;
- care.

```text
shared grammar
≠ identical content
```

The function of the map is to offer an open structure for recognizing relationships, not to impose a standard biography.

---

## 19. Reading criteria

The following questions can be used to work with the map:

1. What relationships have sustained this experience?
2. What antecedents continue to act?
3. What territory has shaped the gaze?
4. What systems expand or limit capacity?
5. What crises changed the trajectory?
6. What tools participate?
7. What decisions produce effects?
8. What purpose provides orientation?
9. What must remain under stewardship?
10. What can return to the territory?

These questions do not seek to close an interpretation. They open a situated reading.

---

## 20. Limits

This document does not:

- reduce a person to their background;
- turn the map into a psychological diagnosis;
- establish automatic causalities;
- claim that every layer carries the same weight;
- present a fixed identity;
- replace territorial verification;
- turn genealogy into a profiling tool;
- authorize the extraction of personal data.

Situated genealogy must preserve limits of exposure and stewardship.
