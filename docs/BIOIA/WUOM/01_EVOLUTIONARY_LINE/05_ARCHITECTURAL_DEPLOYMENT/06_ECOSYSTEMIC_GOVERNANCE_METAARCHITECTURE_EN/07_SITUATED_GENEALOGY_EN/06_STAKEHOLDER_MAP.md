# 06 · STAKEHOLDER MAP
## BIOIA / WUOM

**Version:** v1.0  
**Status:** Conceptual framework  
**Language:** English

---

# Purpose

The **BIOIA / WUOM Stakeholder Map** organizes a territory not according to institutional hierarchies, but according to the function each stakeholder performs within the processes of observation, decision, impact, and return.

Its objective is not to rank who is more important, but to show how the following circulate:

- information;
- knowledge;
- decisions;
- resources;
- impacts;
- capacities that return—or do not return—to the territory.

The proposal begins with a simple idea:

> **The territory is not just another stakeholder. It is the physical and living foundation that sustains every stakeholder.**

---

# Central principle

BIOIA proposes a partial inversion of the usual reading.

Instead of beginning with institutions or decision-making centers, it begins with the territory and reconstructs the network of relationships from there.

The question is no longer:

> Who holds power?

It becomes four complementary questions:

- Who observes?
- Who decides?
- Who receives the impact?
- Who returns capacities to the territory?

---

# The four layers of the system

## LAYER 1 · Territorial foundation

This is the physical support of the system.

It includes, among other elements:

- soil
- water
- temperature
- biodiversity
- ecological time
- landscape
- natural processes

It does not make decisions.

It does not produce policies.

But it makes it possible for all of them to have consequences.

Without this foundation, no territorial system exists.

---

## LAYER 2 · Situated stakeholders

These are those who maintain an everyday relationship with the territory.

For example:

- farmers
- livestock farmers
- inhabitants
- stewards
- local communities
- territorial associations
- small businesses
- water users
- field observers

Their main function is to:

- observe;
- experience;
- detect changes;
- verify impacts.

They constitute the first source of situated experience.

---

## LAYER 3 · Knowledge, stewardship, and mediation

This is where experience is interpreted.

Participants include, among others:

- field technicians
- ecologists
- universities
- research centers
- environmental organizations
- professional associations
- facilitators
- GIS systems
- situated artificial intelligence

Their function is to:

- relate information;
- produce knowledge;
- verify hypotheses;
- support decisions.

In BIOIA, AI belongs to this layer.

It does not replace the territory.

It amplifies reading capacity.

---

## LAYER 4 · Decision, economy, and network

This is the layer with the greatest formal capacity for action.

It includes:

### Public decision-making

- municipalities
- regional administrations
- state administrations
- European bodies
- public policies

### Economic activity

- companies
- developers
- consultancies
- agri-food sector
- tourism
- infrastructure

### Network and funding

- financial institutions
- funds
- grants
- digital platforms
- media
- collaboration networks

This layer concentrates much of the following:

- resources;
- funding;
- regulatory capacity;
- implementation capacity.

At the same time, it is usually further removed from direct territorial experience.

---

# Three fundamental flows

## 1. Upward extraction of information

Information moves upward from the territory.

It includes:

- observations;
- data;
- resources;
- local knowledge;
- experience;
- detected impacts.

This is the flow that feeds the rest of the system.

---

## 2. Territorial return

This is the flow that returns capacities.

It may take different forms:

- useful funding;
- restoration;
- knowledge;
- tools;
- technical capacities;
- verifiable decisions.

When return exists, the system strengthens the territory.

---

## 3. Incomplete return

Not every decision truly returns to the place where the information originated.

It may be lost in:

- procedures;
- bureaucracy;
- administrative scales;
- sectoral interests;
- institutional changes.

The result is a partial or nonexistent return.

BIOIA represents this phenomenon through a discontinuous flow.

---

# The Situated Node

At the center of the map appears the **Situated Node**.

Its formula is:

> **Territory + Experience + AI + Return**

It does not represent an institution.

It represents a way of working.

Its function is to integrate:

- territorial experience;
- scientific knowledge;
- artificial intelligence;
- verifiable return.

The Situated Node connects the different layers without replacing any of them.

---

# Territorial distance

The map also represents an important difference.

As one moves upward:

- formal power increases;
- financial capacity increases;
- distance from the territory increases.

As one moves downward:

- situated experience increases;
- direct observation increases;
- exposure to impacts increases.

BIOIA considers it necessary to connect both dimensions through processes of return.

---

# Territory as foundation

The territory appears as living infrastructure.

It is not merely landscape.

It is a system composed of multiple processes that interact continuously.

Among them:

- soil;
- water;
- biodiversity;
- temperature;
- ecological cycles;
- time.

Every stakeholder depends on this foundation, although not all maintain the same relationship with it.

---

# Change of approach

In many conventional models, stakeholders are ordered according to:

- power;
- influence;
- interest.

BIOIA incorporates another dimension:

**responsibility for return.**

Making decisions is not enough.

The following must also be evaluated:

- who returns capacities;
- who maintains the relationship with the territory;
- who assumes the effects produced.

---

# Key idea

The map proposes understanding territory as a relational system in which information rises from situated experience and decisions are completed only when they produce a verifiable return.

The network can expand capacities, coordinate stakeholders, and facilitate resources, but it cannot replace the physical reality of the territory.

BIOIA therefore summarizes this principle through a central statement:

> **The network does not replace the territory.**

And an operational consequence:

> **Without return, there is extraction.**
