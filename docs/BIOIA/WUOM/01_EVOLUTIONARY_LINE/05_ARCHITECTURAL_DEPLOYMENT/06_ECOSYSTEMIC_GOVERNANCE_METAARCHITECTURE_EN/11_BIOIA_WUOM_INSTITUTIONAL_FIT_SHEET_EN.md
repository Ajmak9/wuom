## 1. Purpose

This sheet makes it possible to assess whether a university, research centre, public administration, foundation, territorial entity, consortium or funding call contains a real space for collaboration with BIOIA / WUOM.

It does not seek to adapt BIOIA to an external opportunity.

It seeks to determine whether the opportunity is already compatible with:

- BIOIA's territorial orientation;
- its relational architecture;
- custody of its corpus;
- non-appropriation;
- protection of sensitive information;
- the existence of verifiable territorial return.

> BIOIA is not modified in order to enter a funding call.  
> The question is whether the call can relate to BIOIA without deforming it.

This document does not constitute a funding application, a commercial offer or a closed contractual proposal.

---

## 2. What BIOIA / WUOM Is

**BIOIA** is a cognitive organism and an architecture of situated orientation that relates:

> TERRITORY + EXPERIENCE + AI + RETURN

Its function is not to replace human decision-making or automate the territory.

Its function is to help:

- read dispersed signals;
- relate scales, actors and consequences;
- recognize limits;
- organize situated information;
- orient decisions;
- determine whether knowledge and action produce return towards the common basis.

**WUOM** brings together reading instruments and structures that may be used when necessary.

The relationship between them is:

> BIOIA is the organism.  
> WUOM provides reading and orientation tools.

BIOIA remains the general framework. WUOM is activated proportionally to each situation.

## 3. Problem Addressed

Many environmental, scientific, technological and administrative projects work with fragmented information.

The following are often observed separately:

- data;
- species;
- infrastructure;
- energy;
- water;
- health;
- climate;
- economy;
- land uses;
- local knowledge;
- institutional decisions;
- territorial consequences.

Fragmentation may produce decisions that are technically correct within one layer but insufficient or contradictory when the complete ecosystem is considered.

BIOIA addresses that discontinuity through a relational reading:

> signal → context → relations → orientation → return

The question is not only what can be done.

It is also necessary to ask:

- From where is the observation made?
- What basis sustains the intervention?
- Who defines its purpose?
- Who receives the benefit?
- Who absorbs the cost?
- Which capacities are preserved or lost?
- What returns to the territory?

## 4. What BIOIA Can Contribute

BIOIA can contribute the following to an institutional collaboration.

### 4.1. Ecosystemic Orientation Framework

An architecture for relating territory, experience, knowledge, artificial intelligence, institutions and material consequences.

### 4.2. Situated Territorial Reading

Capacity to incorporate local signals, territorial memory, direct observation and relations that do not always appear in databases or sectoral administrative files.

### 4.3. Relational Methodology

Protocols for connecting scientific, administrative, technological and experiential information without collapsing one layer into another.

### 4.4. Identification of Actors and Responsibilities

Reading of:

- who observes;
- who knows;
- who decides;
- who executes;
- who finances;
- who receives the impact;
- who verifies;
- who returns capacities to the territory.

### 4.5. Return Assessment

Criteria for determining whether an action returns ecological, social, cognitive or institutional capacities to the basis from which it obtains value.

> Without return, there is extraction.

### 4.6. Situated Cases

BIOIA can work through concrete, traceable and territorially verifiable cases without turning them into merely decorative examples.

### 4.7. Interface Between Knowledge and Decision

A way of organizing signals and relations before acting while keeping deliberation and responsibility with people and institutions.

## 5. What BIOIA Is Not

BIOIA is not:

- a startup;
- a conventional technology application;
- a generalist consultancy;
- a data-extraction platform;
- a surveillance system;
- a profiling tool;
- a brand available for absorption by another entity;
- an interchangeable methodology without authorship or context;
- a pilot territory separated from its inhabitants and relations;
- an automatic solution that replaces deliberation;
- an architecture subordinated to the requirements of a funding call.

BIOIA also does not present artificial intelligence as an autonomous purpose.

AI may read, relate, organize and amplify capacities. It must not replace collective orientation or turn the territory into a passive resource.

## 7. Minimum Fit Conditions

An institutional relationship can be considered compatible only when it meets, at minimum, the following conditions.

### 7.1. Territory Occupies a Structural Position

The territory does not appear only as a location, data source, demonstration setting or final recipient of impacts.

It must be recognized as the living, material and relational basis of the project.

### 7.2. Situated Experience Retains Its Function

Local knowledge and territorial observation cannot be reduced to auxiliary information automatically subordinated to external models.

### 7.3. AI Retains a Proportional Function

Artificial intelligence may support reading, relating, detection and information organization.

It must not replace human deliberation, institutional responsibility or territorial verification.

### 7.4. Verifiable Return Exists

The project must identify which capacities it returns to the territory and how that return will be verified.

### 7.5. Authorship and Custody Are Recognized

The collaboration must respect:

- the BIOIA / WUOM designation;
- attribution;
- the genealogy of the corpus;
- its licence;
- its limits of use;
- custody capacity over its evolution.

### 7.6. No Appropriation or Relabelling Occurs

BIOIA cannot be absorbed, renamed, fragmented or presented as the exclusive creation of a collaborating entity.

### 7.7. Sensitive Data Are Protected

Territorial, personal, ecological or institutional documentation must be used proportionally, traceably and only for the agreed purpose.

### 7.8. The Eligible Entity Assumes Its Responsibility

The university, centre, administration or coordinating entity must assume the scientific, legal, economic and administrative responsibility corresponding to it.

BIOIA does not replace the formal structure required by a funding call.

## 8. Institutional Fit Questions

Before proceeding, the institution or project should be able to answer clearly:

1. What shared problem does it seek to address?
2. Why is BIOIA relevant to that problem?
3. What specific function would BIOIA perform?
4. Which part of the corpus would be used?
5. How would authorship, attribution and custody be recognized?
6. What territorial information would be required?
7. What verifiable return would the territory receive?
8. Who would assume institutional and administrative responsibilities?
9. Which results would be public and which would require protection?
10. Can BIOIA withdraw if the conditions of compatibility disappear?

The absence of clear answers indicates that sufficient fit does not yet exist.

---

## 9. Indicators of Incompatibility

An opportunity must be rejected or kept under observation when it:

- requires BIOIA to become a conventional technology product;
- places financing above orientation;
- uses the territory only as a data source;
- requires transfer of the corpus without custody safeguards;
- reduces BIOIA to a brand, narrative or communication layer;
- eliminates situated experience;
- substitutes institutional visibility for return;
- imposes deliverables incompatible with its nature;
- requires acceptance of surveillance, profiling, propaganda or extraction;
- presents as collaboration what would materially be appropriation.

> Expanding scope does not mean expanding extraction.

## 10. Possible Results of a Compatible Collaboration

A well-oriented collaboration could produce:

- a relational territorial diagnosis;
- a map of signals, actors and responsibilities;
- an observation and verification protocol;
- a documented situated case;
- an ecosystemic-governance methodology;
- a limited pilot for AI-assisted reading;
- impact- and return-assessment criteria;
- attributed public documentation;
- institutional learning;
- capacities that remain in the territory after the project.

The result must not be exhausted by a publication, platform, administrative file or final report.

It must leave reading, orientation and continuity capacity behind.

## 12. Final Criterion

BIOIA can broaden its scope through compatible relationships.

It does not need to transform itself in order to become fundable, academic or institutionally recognizable.

The starting question is not:

> How do we make BIOIA fit this funding call?

The correct question is:

> Does this institution, project or funding call contain a real space for BIOIA without BIOIA ceasing to be BIOIA?

When the answer is affirmative, the following must also exist:

> clear orientation + custody + responsibility + verifiable territorial return
