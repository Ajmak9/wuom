## 1. Overview

The **Fire Preinterface BIOIA / WUOM** evaluates whether fire constitutes a territorial dimension with sufficient autonomy to become a consolidated interface within the system.

Fire is not incorporated as a stable organ at this stage.

It remains a **strong preinterface** because it has surpassed the candidate-tree phase, has passed an initial methodological audit, and shows enough territorial and operational autonomy to require further evaluation.

Its central hypothesis is that fire cannot be reduced to heat, combustion, ecological damage, social emergency, or post-fire degradation.

Fire may constitute a distinct reading organ because it reveals a **territorial threshold of transformation** where multiple conditions converge and change state simultaneously.

---

## 2. Foundational Question

> **Does fire express its own territorial organization, or is it only a consequence of other processes already interpreted by the system?**

This question protects the architecture from adding a new interface merely because a phenomenon is visible or socially significant.

The preinterface must demonstrate that it can read something that the ten consolidated interfaces cannot sufficiently organize on their own.

---

## 3. Purpose

The purpose of the Fire Preinterface is to explore whether fire can be interpreted as a territorial threshold where the following dimensions converge:

- temperature;
- water;
- soil;
- vegetation;
- biodiversity;
- energy;
- connectivity;
- time;
- community;
- communication;
- genetic adaptation;
- wind;
- rural abandonment;
- territorial management;
- vulnerability;
- response capacity;
- regeneration;
- territorial return.

The objective is not to create an interface devoted only to wildfires or emergency response.

The objective is to determine whether fire provides a distinct and operationally useful way of reading territorial transformation.

---

## 4. Initial Hypothesis

Fire is not only a source of heat.

It appears when multiple territorial conditions couple and cross a critical threshold.

These conditions may include:

- accumulated heat;
- low humidity;
- vegetation stress;
- available fuel;
- continuity of combustible material;
- wind;
- topography;
- land use;
- human pressure;
- management or abandonment;
- ecological vulnerability;
- response capacity;
- accumulated temporal processes.

No single variable explains fire by itself.

The preinterface therefore examines fire as a relational phenomenon rather than as an isolated event.

---

## 5. Proposed Organizing Center

> **Fire organizes the transition between territorial stability and territorial transformation.**

This organizing center is provisional but operational.

Fire may express:

- accumulation;
- tension;
- fragility;
- release of energy;
- rupture;
- propagation;
- loss of continuity;
- regeneration;
- irreversible change;
- change of territorial state.

The preinterface does not study fire only as destruction.

It studies the threshold through which multiple territorial conditions are reorganized.

## 6. What Fire May Make Legible

When fire is used as a criterion of territorial reading, it may reveal:

- degree of territorial vulnerability;
- continuity of combustible material;
- condition of the landscape;
- effects of prolonged abandonment;
- hydrological memory;
- soil exposure;
- ecological resilience;
- prevention capacity;
- collective response capacity;
- propagation pathways;
- territorial discontinuities;
- post-fire regeneration potential;
- accumulated temporal pressure;
- relationships between ecological dynamics and human management.

The fire event is not the final object of study.

The territory is the object of study.

Fire may become a way of making that territory legible.

## 7. Relationship with the Ten Consolidated Interfaces

Fire is related to all ten consolidated interfaces, but it is not fully absorbed by any single one.

### 7.1 Temperature

Temperature influences the probability of fire by increasing:

- dryness;
- vegetation stress;
- atmospheric demand;
- territorial flammability.

However, extreme heat does not necessarily produce fire.

Temperature prepares conditions.

Fire expresses a change of state.

### 7.2 Water

Water regulates moisture in:

- soil;
- vegetation;
- atmosphere;
- water bodies;
- ecological refuges.

Water scarcity increases vulnerability.

Available water may reduce risk, slow propagation, or support recovery.

Fire can therefore reveal the hydrological memory of the territory.

### 7.3 Soil

Soil influences:

- moisture retention;
- vegetation development;
- fuel conditions;
- resilience;
- post-fire recovery.

After fire, soil may experience:

- loss of organic matter;
- erosion;
- hydrophobicity;
- compaction;
- loss of biological life;
- increased runoff;
- reduced regeneration capacity.

Fire reveals both prior vulnerability and subsequent recovery potential.

### 7.4 Biodiversity

Biodiversity influences vegetation structure, fuel continuity, ecological redundancy, and resilience.

Fire may:

- destroy habitats;
- reduce species continuity;
- interrupt ecological processes;
- trigger regeneration in fire-adapted systems;
- alter the composition of living communities.

The BIOIA / WUOM question is not whether fire is universally good or bad.

The question is what relationship a specific fire process maintains with the living continuity of the territory.

### 7.5 Community

Community participates in:

- prevention;
- surveillance;
- landscape management;
- emergency response;
- evacuation;
- mutual support;
- post-fire recovery;
- territorial memory.

Fire may express forms of:

- care;
- abandonment;
- pressure;
- occupation;
- rural disconnection;
- collective preparedness;
- unequal vulnerability.

### 7.6 Energy

Fire involves the rapid release of accumulated energy.

The preinterface distinguishes among:

- stored energy;
- released energy;
- destructive energy;
- regenerative processes;
- social energy mobilized for response;
- technological energy used for containment and recovery.

Fire is not reduced to combustion.

It reads how accumulated territorial energy crosses a transformation threshold.

### 7.7 Connectivity

Territorial connectivity influences propagation.

Relevant factors include:

- continuity of vegetation;
- ecological corridors;
- roads;
- paths;
- firebreaks;
- infrastructures;
- settlements;
- landscape discontinuities.

Fire may reveal existing connections and ruptures.

It may also break ecological, social, and functional continuity.

### 7.8 Time

Fire occurs at a specific moment but responds to longer temporal accumulations.

It may reveal:

- previous droughts;
- prolonged abandonment;
- fuel accumulation;
- changes in land management;
- seasonality;
- delayed prevention;
- response speed;
- recovery time;
- repetition of extreme events.

Fire is not only an event.

It may be the visible expression of accumulated territorial time.

### 7.9 Genetics

The relationship with Genetics is indirect but relevant in specific cases.

It may include:

- adaptation of species to fire;
- resistance or vulnerability;
- post-fire regeneration;
- selection pressures;
- loss of genetic diversity;
- changes in population continuity.

Genetics is not central to defining Fire, but it may contribute to situated interpretation.

### 7.10 Communication

Communication participates in:

- alerts;
- prevention;
- public orientation;
- coordination;
- risk interpretation;
- emergency response;
- post-fire memory;
- knowledge transfer.

Poor communication may amplify damage.

Responsible communication may strengthen prevention, response, and recovery.

## 8. Transversal Reading

The singularity of Fire may lie in its capacity to read the moment when multiple territorial conditions move together:

```text
Temperature prepares
Water modulates
Soil sustains or becomes exposed
Biodiversity conditions and responds
Community prevents, suffers and recovers
Energy is released
Connectivity propagates or interrupts
Time accumulates
Communication orients
Genetics adapts or loses continuity
        ↓
Fire transforms
```

The preinterface does not duplicate these interfaces.

It investigates their convergence at a territorial threshold.

---

## 9. Operational Flow

The provisional operational flow is:

```text
Observe territorial conditions
        ↓

        ↓
Identify accumulated pressures
        ↓
Recognize threshold conditions
        ↓
Interpret potential transformation
        ↓

        ↓

```

The flow must remain situated.

It does not authorize automated prediction or direct intervention.

## 12. Main Methodological Risk

The principal risk is reducing Fire to an interface devoted only to wildfires, heat, combustion, or emergency management.

BIOIA / WUOM must preserve the broader formulation:

> **Fire as a territorial threshold of transformation.**

The preinterface must not become:

- a wildfire prediction system;
- an emergency protocol;
- a firefighting tool;
- a fire-risk map;
- a substitute for forestry, ecology, civil protection, engineering, or public administration.

Its role is to organize a territorial reading.

---

## 13. Scope

The Fire Preinterface may:

- interpret convergence among multiple territorial conditions;
- identify potential thresholds of transformation;
- relate vulnerability, continuity, accumulation, propagation, rupture, and regeneration;
- support situated prevention and restoration thinking;
- contribute to territorial planning;
- compare pre-fire and post-fire conditions;
- organize return among the consolidated interfaces;
- preserve uncertainty;
- remain open to correction through evidence.

---

## 14. Limits

The Fire Preinterface does not:

- predict ignition;
- calculate fire behaviour;
- model propagation;
- define evacuation plans;
- manage emergencies;
- prescribe forest management;
- design firebreaks;
- replace field observation;
- replace professional fire science;
- replace ecological assessment;
- replace civil protection;
- replace public responsibility;
- authorize territorial action;
- classify every fire as destructive;
- assume the same fire dynamics in every ecosystem;
- consolidate itself automatically as an interface.

Its conclusions must remain proportional to available evidence.

BIOIA / WUOM recognizes the following methodological sequence:

```text
Candidate Tree
        ↓
Preinterface
        ↓
Consolidated Interface
```

Each transition requires:

- territorial evidence;
- a stable organizing center;
- operational utility;
- non-substitution;
- acceptable overlap;
- verification through return;
- cross-interface review.

The second circle exists to prevent premature expansion.

It protects the system from growing by accumulation and protects candidates from being consolidated before they have matured.
