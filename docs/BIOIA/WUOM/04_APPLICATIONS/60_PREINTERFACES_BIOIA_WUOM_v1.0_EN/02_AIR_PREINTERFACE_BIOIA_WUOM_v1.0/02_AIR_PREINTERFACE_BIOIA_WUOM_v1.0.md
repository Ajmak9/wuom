---

## 1. Definition

The **Air Pre-interface** explores whether air constitutes a territorial dimension with an identity of its own within BIOIA / WUOM.

Air is not understood only as atmosphere, environmental quality or wind.

It is read as the **invisible shared medium in which exchanges occur between territory, living beings, physical processes and human activity**.

---

## 2. Purpose

To assess whether air has sufficient methodological autonomy, operational usefulness and territorial relevance to evolve, in the future, into a consolidated interface.

The Air Pre-interface reads the convergence of:

- respiration;
- particles;
- aerosols;
- humidity;
- pollen;
- smoke;
- dust;
- odors;
- gases;
- emissions;
- circulation;
- public health;
- biodiversity;
- temperature;
- human activity.

Its purpose is not merely to measure air, but to understand the role of air within the ecosystem and the territory.

---

## 3. Foundational Question

> **Does air express a territorial organization of its own, or is it only a variable derived from other processes in the system?**

A second validation question follows:

> **What can Air read that cannot be read sufficiently through the ten consolidated interfaces or through the Wind Tree?**

---

## 4. Fundamental Principle

> **Air is not empty. It is a living medium of relation.**

Air does not separate territorial processes.

It allows them to breathe, circulate, mix, exchange and become perceptible within one shared medium.

---

## 5. Provisional Organizing Center

> **Air functions as the invisible medium of respiration, exchange, exposure and territorial relation.**

This organizing center is provisionally stable, but it still requires continued territorial validation.

---

## 6. Function

The Air Pre-interface helps to:

- observe how air participates in territorial organization;
- read processes of respiration, circulation and exchange;
- identify invisible relationships between organisms and environment;
- connect physical, biological, chemical, atmospheric and social processes;
- distinguish isolated episodes from recurrent or accumulated conditions;
- detect exposure, stagnation, renewal, dispersion and transport;
- relate air signals to the consolidated interfaces;
- support situated orientation through territorial return.

---

## 7. Reading Criteria

### 7.1 Respiration

Who breathes this air?

- people;
- fauna;
- flora;
- microorganisms.

### 7.2 Composition

What does the air contain?

- particles;
- aerosols;
- pollen;
- smoke;
- dust;
- humidity;
- gases;
- odors.

### 7.3 Circulation

How does air circulate?

The reading is not limited to wind. It also considers:

- renewal;
- stagnation;
- exchange;
- natural ventilation;
- accumulation;
- dispersion.

### 7.4 Relational Reading

Air is never interpreted in isolation.

Its signals must be connected with the interfaces and with relevant candidate trees or pre-interfaces.

### 7.5 Temporal Continuity

Is the signal:

- an isolated episode;
- a recurrent pattern;
- an accumulated condition;
- a structural change?

BIOIA / WUOM gives priority to continued observation over isolated data.

### 7.6 Signals

Relevant signals may include:

- persistent smoke;
- unusual dust;
- repeated odors;
- fog;
- increased particles;
- seasonal pollen;
- heavy or stagnant air;
- changes in atmospheric transparency;
- humidity changes;
- recurrent exposure.

### 7.7 Territorial Return

Every reading must return to the territory.

Guiding questions include:

- What is happening here?
- Who or what is affected?
- Which relationships become visible?
- Which interfaces help to understand the situation?
- Does the reading improve orientation?

## 9. What Air Does Not Do

The Air Pre-interface:

- does not replace meteorology;
- does not replace official environmental-quality systems;
- does not perform medical diagnoses;
- does not interpret climate by itself;
- does not replace regulatory monitoring;
- does not replace technical measurement;
- does not replace the Wind Tree.

Its function is ecological, relational and territorial.

---

## 10. Difference Between Air and Wind

Air and Wind are related, but they do not describe the same phenomenon.

> **Air is the medium.**  
> **Wind is the movement of the medium.**

Wind can move smoke, particles, humidity, pollen or odors.

Air remains the medium in which these elements exist, mix, circulate and affect living beings.

Current architectural status:

- **Air:** active pre-interface in advanced evaluation;
- **Wind:** candidate tree remaining in the nursery.

This distinction protects the architecture from conceptual overlap.

## 11. Relationship with the Ten Consolidated Interfaces

### 11.1 Temperature

Air transports and accumulates heat.

Temperature modifies air density, humidity, movement and its effects on living beings.

Temperature reads the thermal condition.

Air reads the medium in which that condition circulates, is breathed and is experienced.

### 11.2 Water

Air contains humidity and participates in:

- evaporation;
- condensation;
- fog;
- rain;
- dryness;
- thermal sensation.

Air and Water are connected through exchanges between atmosphere, soil, vegetation and living bodies.

### 11.3 Soil

Soil emits, retains and receives particles.

The relationship appears through:

- dust;
- erosion;
- soil humidity;
- organic matter;
- microorganisms;
- suspended particles;
- surface dryness.

Soil conditions modify nearby air conditions.

### 11.4 Biodiversity

Air enables respiration, dispersal and chemical communication.

It affects:

- pollen;
- seeds;
- insects;
- birds;
- sensitive fauna;
- fungi;
- odors;
- chemical signals.

Air forms part of the relational space of many living beings.

### 11.5 Community

Communities breathe the air of the territory.

Air is related to:

- health;
- comfort;
- work;
- rest;
- environmental perception;
- exposure to smoke, dust, odors or emissions.

### 11.6 Energy

Energy intervenes through emissions, combustion and atmospheric transformation.

Air is related to:

- fuels;
- industry;
- mobility;
- heating;
- cooling;
- emissions;
- energy demand;
- combustion.

Energy modifies air, and air conditions energy use.

### 11.7 Connectivity

Air connects territories.

It transports:

- particles;
- pollen;
- smoke;
- humidity;
- pollutants;
- odors;
- air masses.

Air reveals relationships that do not follow administrative boundaries.

### 11.8 Time

Air changes through:

- daily cycles;
- seasonal cycles;
- meteorological episodes;
- climatic processes;
- exceptional events.

Time distinguishes an isolated episode from a recurrent pattern, accumulated condition or structural change.

### 11.9 Genetics

The relationship with Genetics is indirect but may appear in:

- species adaptation to atmospheric conditions;
- differential sensitivity to pollen, pollution or dryness;
- genetic dispersal through pollen and seeds;
- selection of more resistant species.

This is not a central relationship for defining Air, but it may become relevant in specific cases.

### 11.10 Communication

Air participates in the transmission and interpretation of signals.

Communication can translate air-related signals into useful information through:

- environmental alerts;
- risk perception;
- communication of smoke or pollution episodes;
- public interpretation of odors, dust or poor air quality;
- community orientation.

---

## 12. Relationship with Fire

Air maintains a direct relationship with the Fire Pre-interface.

Fire modifies air through:

- smoke;
- particles;
- gases;
- heat;
- reduced visibility;
- altered territorial respiration.

Air makes it possible to read the invisible effects of fire beyond the burned area.

## 13. First Territorial Return

### Return Question

> **What territorial information appears when air is used as a reading criterion?**

### Observable Signals

The first return may register:

- smoke;
- dust;
- particles;
- aerosols;
- pollen;
- persistent odors;
- atmospheric humidity;
- heavy-air sensation;
- transparency or turbidity;
- poor-air-quality episodes;
- changes following fires, droughts, rain or wind.

### Possible Application Cases

- pollution episodes;
- wildfire smoke;
- suspended dust;
- industrial or agricultural odors;
- increased pollen;
- stagnant air;
- heat waves with low ventilation;
- persistent fog or humidity;
- relationships between cleaner air, radiation and heat;
- urban and rural habitability.

### Indicators of Usefulness

The Air Pre-interface is useful when it helps identify:

- exposure conditions;
- relationships between air and territorial health;
- connections among emissions, wind, fire and temperature;
- invisible effects on biodiversity and community;
- cumulative episodes;
- changes in environmental perception;
- signals crossing administrative boundaries.

### Indicators of Non-validation

The pre-interface would lose autonomy if it only reproduced readings already provided by:

- Temperature;
- Water;
- Energy;
- Community;
- Fire;
- Wind.

In that case, it should remain a pre-interface or return to the nursery.

## 14. Architectural Position

BIOIA / WUOM currently recognizes three levels:

1. **Consolidated interfaces** — organs of the first operational circle;
2. **Pre-interfaces** — organs under advanced evaluation;
3. **Nursery** — candidate trees under observation.

Air occupies the second level.

The second circle in formation currently includes:

- Fire;
- Air;
- Darkness.

Air is not yet part of the stable operational core.

---

## 15. Methodological Decision

The Air Pre-interface remains active.

It:

- does not return to the nursery;
- does not yet enter the stable interface core;
- remains under advanced observation and territorial validation.

The first evaluation found that Air:

- has a provisional organizing center of its own;
- relates to all consolidated interfaces;
- cannot be reduced only to pollution;
- cannot be reduced only to wind;
- helps interpret invisible territorial processes.

---

## 16. Nomenclature Correction

The initial designation was corrected:

> **Air Quality → Air**

BIOIA / WUOM names trees after territorial phenomena, not after a single property of the phenomenon.

Air quality therefore becomes an internal dimension of the Air Tree rather than its name.

---

## 17. Validation Conditions

Air may advance toward a consolidated interface only if future territorial returns confirm:

- methodological autonomy;
- real operational usefulness;
- a stable organizing center;
- applicability in different contexts;
- continued territorial verification;
- limited redundancy with existing interfaces;
- autonomy from Wind, Temperature, Water and Energy;
- capacity to reveal invisible territorial relationships;
- capacity to improve orientation consistently.

A pre-interface is not consolidated by intuition.

It is consolidated only when it consistently improves territorial reading.

## 19. Provisional Conclusion

The Air Pre-interface shows sufficient identity to continue developing.

Air does not appear to be only a property of other interfaces.

It operates as a shared territorial medium in which physical, biological and social processes converge.

Its value does not lie in replacing the consolidated interfaces.

Its value lies in making visible the medium in which multiple territorial processes breathe, circulate, mix, accumulate and relate.

---

## 20. Synthesis

> **Air is not empty.**  
> **Air is not only wind.**  
> **Air is the invisible medium in which territory breathes, exchanges and relates.**

> **Air does not organize the territory by itself.**  
> **It makes it possible for many territorial processes to connect, exchange and become visible as one shared medium.**
