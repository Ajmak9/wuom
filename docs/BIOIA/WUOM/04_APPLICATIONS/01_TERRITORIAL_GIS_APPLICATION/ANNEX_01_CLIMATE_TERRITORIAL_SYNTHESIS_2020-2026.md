## Purpose

Annex 01 introduces an applied climate-territorial reading based on official climate data.

It shows how a territorial GIS-oriented framework can support the transformation of climate records into a readable territorial synthesis.

This annex functions as:

* an applied demonstration case
* a public-facing synthesis layer
* a climate-territory reading example
* a bridge between data, GIS and territorial interpretation

---

## Data Source

Source:

**AEMET OpenData**

Reference station:

**Tortosa / Roquetes [9981A]**

Period:

**2020-01-01 to 2026-06-12**

Main variables:

* mean temperature
* maximum temperature
* minimum temperature
* precipitation

---

## Reading Focus

The annex focuses on:

* annual mean temperature
* tropical nights
* days with maximum temperature ≥ 35 ºC
* meteorological summer intensity
* accumulated precipitation
* maximum daily precipitation
* partial-year comparison
* rainfall before summer
* territorial response capacity

---

## Main Interpretation

The climate-territorial synthesis identifies a relevant transition:

**2025 concentrates the strongest thermal signal.**

**2026 enters the summer season with more accumulated rainfall than 2025 at the same date.**

This does not remove the thermal pressure.

It modifies the hydric base with which the territory enters the warm season.

---

## Methodological Caution

AEMET 9981A is used as an official reference station for the Tortosa / Roquetes area.

It should not be presented as a direct measurement of a private site, protected field point or local microclimate.

Correct formulation:

**according to AEMET 9981A, within the Tortosa / Roquetes reference area, for the available period.**

## Core Formula

Official data becomes territorial reading when it is placed in relation with:

* time
* scale
* climate sequence
* field context
* territorial response
* careful interpretation

Data alone is not orientation.

Relation, context and verification make it readable.
