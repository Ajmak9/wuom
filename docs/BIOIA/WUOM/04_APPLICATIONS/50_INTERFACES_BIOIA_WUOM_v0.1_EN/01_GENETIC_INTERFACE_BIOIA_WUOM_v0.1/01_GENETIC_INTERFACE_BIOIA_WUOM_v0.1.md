## 1. What Is the Genetic Interface?

The **Genetic Interface** is an operational instrument within BIOIA/WUOM designed to incorporate the genetic dimension into processes of territorial reading and situated decision-making.

It is not a conceptual tree.

It is not a structural document.

It is an **interface**: a mechanism that transforms structured reading into operational orientation without altering the architecture of the system.

Its function is to integrate the genetic dimension—understood broadly—into territorial orientation whenever that dimension is relevant to decision-making.

> **The Genetic Interface does not study genetics.**
>
> **It incorporates the genetic dimension into territorial orientation.**

---

## 2. The Genetic Dimension

Within BIOIA/WUOM, the genetic dimension is understood as a property of living systems related to:

- diversity;
- variability;
- heredity;
- adaptation;
- resilience.

The Genetic Interface does not replace specialized biological disciplines and does not seek to analyze the genome.

Its role is to incorporate this dimension into the ecosystemic reading of the territory whenever it provides useful criteria for situated orientation.

---

## 3. Purpose

The purpose of the Genetic Interface is to **incorporate the genetic dimension into BIOIA/WUOM territorial orientation**, transforming the reading of that dimension into operational guidance that can support situated, coherent, and proportional decisions.

Its function is not to analyze the genome or replace biological expertise.

Its function is to incorporate the genetic dimension when it provides relevant signals for territorial orientation.

> **The Genetic Interface transforms the reading of the genetic dimension into operational orientation.**

The interface exists because the genetic dimension affects:

- biological diversity;
- variability;
- adaptive capacity;
- resilience;
- population stability;
- response to disturbance;
- the evolution of communities and ecosystems.

This dimension is often absent from territorial orientation processes, not because it lacks importance, but because there are few mechanisms for integrating it operationally.

The Genetic Interface addresses that gap.

---

## 4. Relationship with the Genetics Tree

The **🌳 Genetics Tree** belongs to the BIOIA/WUOM Tree Family.

Its function is to:

- map the genetic dimension;
- organize its reading;
- identify relevant variables, relationships, and signals.

The Genetic Interface performs the next operation:

> **It transforms the structured reading of the genetic dimension into operational orientation for situated decisions.**

The relationship is direct and complementary:

> **The tree is the map.**
>
> **The interface is the instrument used to navigate that map.**

The interface does not replace the tree.

It extends its operational capacity.

## 6. Internal Architecture

The internal architecture of the Genetic Interface is organized into four components:

1. Reception of genetic reading.
2. Cognitive integration.
3. Generation of operational orientation.
4. Delivery to situated applications.

Each component has a specific and limited function.

### 6.1 Reception of Genetic Reading

The interface receives the reading organized by the **🌳 Genetics Tree**.

This input may include:

- signals of genetic diversity;
- indicators of variability;
- patterns of heredity;
- adaptation patterns;
- elements of resilience;
- relationships with other territorial dimensions.

The interface does not generate genetic reading.

It does not operate on raw genetic data.

> **The Genetic Interface begins where the reading of the Genetics Tree ends.**

### 6.2 Cognitive Integration

The genetic reading is integrated into the BIOIA/WUOM framework through:

- the Cognitive Architecture;
- the BIOIA/WUOM Principles;
- the Law of Territorial Return.

This stage performs three functions:

- situated interpretation within the territorial context;
- selection of signals with operational relevance;
- cognitive proportionality in relation to other territorial dimensions.

The genetic dimension is interpreted alongside:

- water;
- soil;
- biodiversity;
- community;
- climate;
- time.

This stage does not create new principles or criteria.

It organizes the reading within the existing framework.

### 6.3 Generation of Operational Orientation

The integrated reading is transformed into operational criteria that may support situated decisions.

These criteria may include:

- recommendations proportional to genetic diversity;
- risk signals associated with loss of variability;
- adaptive management criteria based on resilience;
- priorities derived from adaptive capacity;
- alerts related to population stability.

This component:

- does not decide;
- does not execute;
- does not act.

> **The interface orients. Applications act.**

Operational orientation must remain:

- situated;
- proportional;
- coherent with the territory;
- verifiable through territorial return.

### 6.4 Delivery to Situated Applications

The orientation generated by the interface may be incorporated into applications such as:

- ecological restoration;
- adaptive management;
- territorial planning;
- land stewardship;
- ecosystem governance.

The interface does not control those applications.

It does not replace their processes.

It does not determine their decisions.

It provides operational criteria that may be integrated according to the nature of each application.

## 7. Operational Flow

The minimum operational flow is:

```text
Reception of genetic reading
        ↓
Cognitive integration
        ↓
Generation of operational orientation
        ↓
Delivery to situated applications
        ↓
Territorial return
```

This is not a rigid protocol.

It is an initial flow designed to evolve through practice and territorial return.

The interface operates only on **structured reading**.

It does not interpret raw genetic information.

## 8. Initial Fields of Application

The fields of application are initial contexts in which the genetic dimension may add value to territorial orientation.

They are not exhaustive.

They are not fully developed applications.

They are not yet validated cases.

### 8.1 Ecological Restoration

The interface may support orientation related to:

- selection of sources of biological material;
- available genetic diversity;
- intraspecific variability;
- signals of local adaptation;
- risk associated with loss of variability;
- prioritization of restoration actions;
- adjustment of intervention intensity;
- sequencing of restoration processes.

### 8.2 Adaptive Management

The interface may contribute to:

- identification of vulnerable populations;
- recognition of low diversity or loss of variability;
- adjustment of management strategies;
- interpretation of adaptive capacity;
- evaluation of population stability;
- anticipation of loss of functionality.

### 8.3 Territorial Planning

The interface may support:

- recognition of areas with high genetic value;
- reduction of fragmentation risks;
- reinforcement of functional connectivity;
- assessment of impacts on sensitive populations;
- prioritization of ecological corridors;
- interpretation of genetic flow and dispersal patterns.

### 8.4 Stewardship and Ecosystem Governance

The interface may contribute to:

- stewardship agreements;
- conservation of genetic diversity;
- prevention of loss of variability;
- participatory processes;
- communication of genetic risks and opportunities;
- integration of the genetic dimension into territorial reading;
- verification through territorial return.

## 9. Scope

The Genetic Interface operates in the following areas:

### Situated Orientation

It transforms the reading of the genetic dimension into operational orientation that may support territorial decisions.

### Integration of the Genetic Dimension

When relevant, it incorporates:

- diversity;
- variability;
- heredity;
- adaptation;
- resilience.

### Ecosystemic Reading

It places the genetic dimension in relation to other dimensions of the territory.

The genetic dimension is never interpreted in isolation.

### Integration with Applications

It provides operational orientation for applications without replacing their own functions.

---

## 10. Limits

The Genetic Interface does not perform the following functions.

### It Does Not Analyze Primary Genetic Information

It does not:

- interpret genetic sequences;
- perform molecular analyses;
- replace specialized disciplines.

### It Does Not Generate Genetic Reading

The reading comes from the **🌳 Genetics Tree**.

The interface works exclusively on structured reading.

### It Does Not Make Decisions

The interface orients.

Applications materialize that orientation.

Decisions remain with the responsible human, institutional, or territorial context.

### It Does Not Act Directly on the Territory

It does not execute interventions.

It does not modify living systems.

Its function ends with operational orientation.

### It Does Not Redefine BIOIA/WUOM

It does not modify:

- the Core;
- the Cognitive Architecture;
- the Principles;
- the Law of Territorial Return;
- the Master Plan.

### It Does Not Operate in Isolation

The genetic dimension always forms part of an ecosystemic reading.

### It Does Not Replace Other Interfaces

Its function is complementary.

It does not replace territorial, cognitive, community, biodiversity, or other existing or future interfaces.

---

## 11. Principle of Proportionality

> **The genetic dimension is incorporated only when it provides real value to territorial orientation.**

This means that:

- not every decision requires a genetic reading;
- not every genetic signal is operationally relevant;
- not every genetic reading must become orientation.

The interface avoids both over-dimensioning and under-dimensioning the genetic dimension.

---

## 12. Territorial Return

The effective usefulness of the Genetic Interface is verified through territorial return.

This means that:

- the value of its orientation is evaluated in the territory;
- applications return verification signals;
- the interface may adjust its operation when practice justifies it;
- its architecture evolves only when territorial return demonstrates the need.

> **Without return, there is no orientation.**

## 13. Operational Glossary

### Genetics Tree

Conceptual map within the BIOIA/WUOM Tree Family that organizes the reading of the genetic dimension.

### Genetic Dimension

Property of living systems related to diversity, variability, heredity, adaptation, and resilience.

### Genetic Reading

Structured reading produced by the Genetics Tree and used as input by the interface.

### Cognitive Integration

Process through which genetic reading is incorporated into the BIOIA/WUOM framework.

### Operational Orientation

Criteria that support situated decisions without constituting decisions or actions.

### Territorial Return

Process through which the territory verifies the usefulness of the orientation generated by the system.

### Genetic Variability

Degree of genetic difference within a population.

### Genetic Diversity

Set of genetic variants present in a population or community.

### Local Adaptation

Characteristics that allow a population to respond to the conditions of a specific territory.

### Genetic Resilience

Capacity of a population to maintain functional stability under disturbance.

### Population Stability

Condition in which a population maintains functionality without significant signals of genetic instability.

### Genetic Signal

A reading element that provides relevant information for operational orientation.

### Genetic Proportionality

Criterion according to which the genetic dimension is incorporated only when it provides real value to territorial orientation.
