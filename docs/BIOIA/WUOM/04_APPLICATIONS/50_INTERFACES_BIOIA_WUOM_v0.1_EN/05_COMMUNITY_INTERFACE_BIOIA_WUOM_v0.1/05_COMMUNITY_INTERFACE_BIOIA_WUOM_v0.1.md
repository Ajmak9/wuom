# 05 · COMMUNITY INTERFACE — BIOIA / WUOM v0.1

## Status

**Version:** v0.1  
**Type:** Operational interface  
**Status:** Initial operational documentation  
**System:** BIOIA / WUOM  

---

## 1. Foundational Question

The Community Interface begins with a specific question:

> **How can the organization of human relationships and collective capacities be incorporated into situated territorial orientation without reducing them to individuals, institutions, participation, governance, or administrative structures?**

This question defines the interface’s own domain.

The interface does not focus on:

- isolated individuals;
- formal actors;
- institutions;
- citizen participation;
- governance systems;
- administrative structures;
- applied sociology or anthropology.

Its focus is **community organization** as a territorial dimension through which collective capacity emerges from:

- relationships;
- shared practices;
- forms of coordination;
- social continuity;
- joint action;
- community resilience.

---

## 2. Identity

The **Community Interface** is an operational instrument within BIOIA/WUOM designed to incorporate the community organization of collective action into situated territorial orientation.

It treats community as:

- relational organization;
- collective capacity;
- situated territorial structure;
- a source of orientation.

It does not treat community as:

- an aggregation of individuals;
- an institution;
- a participation mechanism;
- a governance model;
- an administrative unit.

> **Community is organization, not aggregation.**
>
> **Collective capacity emerges from relationships, practices, coordination, and continuity.**

Its organizing center is:

> **The community organization of collective action as a territorial signal.**

---

## 3. Purpose

The purpose of the Community Interface is to incorporate the organization of human relationships and collective capacities into situated territorial orientation in a way that is:

- relational;
- proportional;
- territorial;
- non-substitutive;
- verifiable through territorial return.

Its purpose rests on three principles.

### 3.1 Community Is Organization, Not an Aggregation of People

The interface operates on relational patterns and collective structures rather than isolated individuals.

### 3.2 Community Is Collective Capacity, Not Administrative Structure

The interface works with forms of joint action rather than institutions as its organizing center.

### 3.3 Community Is Territorial, Not Abstract

Community organization is interpreted within the specific territory in which it occurs.

The interface seeks to understand how human relationships are organized to:

- sustain a territory;
- transform it;
- respond to change;
- coordinate action;
- preserve continuity;
- generate situated orientation.

---

## 4. Internal Architecture

The Community Interface is organized into three sequential modules:

1. Community reading.
2. Socioterritorial integration.
3. Situated orientation through community organization.

### 4.1 Community Reading

The interface begins by reading how human relationships organize collective capacity within a territory.

This module interprets:

- forms of community coordination;
- shared practices;
- bonds of social continuity;
- modes of joint action;
- emergent relational structures;
- mechanisms of community coherence.

It does not identify actors as isolated analytical units.

It does not describe institutions as the center of the reading.

Its function is to recognize patterns of community organization.

### 4.2 Socioterritorial Integration

The second module integrates community organization with the other territorial signals of the system.

It relates community organization to:

- territorial dynamics;
- spatial structures;
- social temporalities;
- territorial practices;
- signals received from the Core;
- other relevant territorial dimensions.

This integration is relational rather than cumulative.

It does not replace the functions of Communication, Biodiversity, Genetics, Water, or other interfaces.

### 4.3 Situated Orientation through Community Organization

The third module transforms socioterritorial integration into situated territorial orientation.

Orientation may be based on:

- community coherence;
- stability of shared practices;
- territorial social continuity;
- capacity for joint action;
- community resilience;
- modes of collective organization.

The interface does not prescribe governance.

It does not define participation.

It does not act directly on the territory.

It produces proportional and verifiable orientation derived from community organization.

---

## 5. Operational Flow

The minimum operational flow is:

```text
Community reading
        ↓
Socioterritorial integration
        ↓
Situated orientation
        ↓
Applications and decisions
        ↓
Territorial return
```

This flow begins with forms of community organization rather than individuals, institutions, or formal participation processes.

Each stage depends on the previous one.

Verification remains grounded in territorial return.

---

## 6. Initial Fields of Application

The interface operates when community organization provides relevant information for understanding a territory’s collective capacity.

Its fields are not institutional sectors.

They are forms of community organization.

### 6.1 Community Coordination

The interface may interpret:

- emergent forms of coordination;
- mechanisms of social synchronization;
- situated forms of agreement;
- collective alignment;
- coherence of joint action.

The focus is on how coordination is organized, not on who coordinates.

### 6.2 Shared Practices

The interface may incorporate:

- recurring territorial practices;
- collective habits;
- community routines;
- forms of shared care;
- practices that sustain continuity.

Practice is interpreted as an organizational structure rather than individual activity.

### 6.3 Community Bonds

The interface may interpret:

- reciprocity;
- mutual support;
- social continuity;
- emergent relational structures;
- bonds that sustain territorial coherence.

The bond is understood as a form of organization rather than an isolated interpersonal relationship.

### 6.4 Joint Action

The interface may incorporate:

- modes of community action;
- situated collective responses;
- collective mobilization capacities;
- emergent coordination mechanisms.

Action is interpreted as collective capacity rather than formal participation.

### 6.5 Community Resilience

The interface may interpret:

- community reorganization;
- stability of shared practices;
- territorial social continuity;
- collective adaptation;
- the capacity to maintain coherence under territorial change.

Resilience is understood as a property of community organization rather than a psychological or institutional attribute.

## 7. Operational Criteria

The interface operates according to five initial criteria.

### 7.1 Community Coordination

The interface operates when the community expresses forms of emergent coordination.

This criterion considers:

- social synchronization;
- situated agreement;
- collective alignment;
- coherence of joint action.

### 7.2 Shared Practices

The interface operates when shared practices sustain territorial continuity.

This criterion considers:

- collective habits;
- community routines;
- recurring territorial practices;
- stability of shared behavior.

### 7.3 Community Bonds

The interface operates when relational bonds sustain territorial social coherence.

This criterion considers:

- reciprocity;
- mutual support;
- social continuity;
- emergent relational structures.

### 7.4 Joint Action

The interface operates when the community expresses collective capacity for action.

This criterion considers:

- situated collective responses;
- modes of community action;
- mobilization capacity;
- emergent coordination.

### 7.5 Community Resilience

The interface operates when the community maintains or recovers coherence under territorial change.

This criterion considers:

- reorganization;
- continuity of shared practices;
- territorial social continuity;
- collective adaptation.

---

## 8. Scope

The Community Interface may:

- incorporate community coordination into territorial reading;
- interpret shared practices;
- read community bonds;
- integrate joint action as collective capacity;
- interpret community resilience;
- relate community organization to other territorial dimensions;
- generate situated orientation;
- preserve proportionality and uncertainty;
- maintain verifiability through territorial return.

Its scope remains strictly within the interpretation of community organization as a territorial signal.

---

## 9. Limits

The Community Interface does not:

- identify or classify individual actors;
- analyze personal behavior;
- interpret individual motivations;
- evaluate formal participation;
- design governance structures;
- prescribe decision-making processes;
- define public policy;
- analyze institutions as its organizing center;
- design administrative structures;
- create participation processes;
- manage spaces;
- prescribe interventions;
- act directly on the territory;
- replace the Communication Interface;
- replace the Biodiversity Interface;
- replace the Genetic Interface;
- replace the Water Interface;
- replace future interfaces;
- modify the BIOIA/WUOM architecture.

Community organization may be informed by institutions, communication, participation, and governance.

These elements do not constitute the organizing center of this interface.

---

## 10. Contemporary Community Organization

The Community Interface does not represent an idealized community from the past or a fully digital community.

It represents situated community organization within a contemporary territory, where:

- human relationships;
- local knowledge;
- shared practices;
- territorial experience;
- digital networks;
- technological capacities;

may be articulated without losing territorial rootedness.

Technology may support community organization.

It does not replace the community or the territory.

---

## 11. Relationship with BIOIA/WUOM

The Community Interface is integrated into BIOIA/WUOM without modifying the stabilized architecture.

### Relationship with the Core

The interface:

- receives territorial signals from the Core;
- incorporates community organization as a specific dimension of territorial reading;
- does not modify the Core’s principles, laws, or categories.

### Relationship with the Cognitive Architecture

The interface operates through the existing processes of:

- reading;
- integration;
- orientation.

It does not introduce new cognitive mechanisms or create a parallel architecture.

### Relationship with the Law of Territorial Return

The orientation produced by the interface must remain verifiable through territorial return.

The interface therefore preserves:

- explicit uncertainty;
- traceability of community signals;
- situated verification;
- openness to adjustment.

It does not generate return by itself.

### Relationship with the Master Plan

The interface contributes organized relational information to territorial planning without modifying:

- the structure of the Master Plan;
- its categories;
- its design criteria;
- the general organization of the system.

### Relationship with Other Interfaces

The relationship is complementary and non-substitutive.

Each interface responds to a different foundational question.

The Community Interface responds specifically to:

> **How can the community organization of collective action be incorporated into situated territorial orientation?**

---

## 12. Functional Identity

The Community Interface is characterized by:

- relational organization;
- collective capacity;
- shared practices;
- community bonds;
- joint action;
- social continuity;
- community resilience;
- situated orientation;
- territorial verifiability.

Unlike the Communication Interface, it does not safeguard public expression.

Unlike the Biodiversity Interface, it does not interpret ecological organization.

Unlike the Genetic Interface, it does not interpret biological variation.

Unlike the Water Interface, it does not interpret hydroterritorial dynamics.

Its specific function is:

> **To integrate the community organization of collective action into the construction of situated territorial orientation.**

## 13. Version Status

The **Community Interface BIOIA/WUOM v0.1** is the fifth interface developed through an independent foundational question.

It has:

- its own organizing center;
- a specific functional identity;
- a delimited purpose;
- an initial architecture;
- a defined operational flow;
- initial fields of application;
- explicit limits;
- its own operational criteria;
- a contained relationship with the system.

The interface is formally closed as version v0.1.

Future versions may evolve only when:

- practice reveals new operational needs;
- territorial return confirms adjustments;
- interaction with other interfaces requires new precision;
- accumulated experience stabilizes new criteria.

Its evolution will not proceed through analogy, documentary symmetry, or theoretical expansion.

It will proceed through practice and territorial return.

---

## Closing

The Community Interface reads how collective capacity is organized, integrates that organization into the territory, and produces situated orientation without reducing community to individuals, institutions, participation, or governance.

> **Community is not the sum of individuals.**
>
> **It is the organization that makes collective capacity possible.**

> **Without custody, there is no publication.**
>
> **Without return, there is no orientation.**
