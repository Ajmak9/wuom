# DIEGO MEMORANDUM

**BIOIA / WUOM · English technical version · v0.1 · July 2026**

> **Political time can be postponed. Territorial time cannot.**
>
> Every additional interval granted without changing direction prolongs the trajectory, accumulates costs, and reduces the margin available to the next decision.

## 2. Nature and purpose

The Diego Memorandum is a situated civic monitoring document.

It is not:

- a complaint;
- a lawsuit;
- a legal opinion;
- an institutional impact assessment;
- a substitute for the memoranda, positions, or technical analyses that public authorities, organisations, parliamentary groups, scientific teams, legal teams, or economic actors may be required to produce.

Its purpose is to place a European regulatory proposal inside the material climatic and territorial conditions in which it is being processed.

Each recipient or actor must develop its own assessment, position, or memorandum from its own sphere of responsibility.

The Diego Memorandum records:

- the document under discussion;
- the material context in which it appears;
- public reactions;
- measured public silences;
- verified territorial effects;
- changes in institutional response;
- and the later comparison between what was known, what was proposed, what occurred during the interval, and how the final decision was presented.

---

## 3. Why the name “Diego”

**Diego** is an operational name for the monitoring thread.

It does not designate:

- a legal category;
- a responsible individual;
- a public official;
- a political group;
- or a causal agent.

The name identifies a recurring governance operation:

> additional political time is requested while the material trajectory remains active.

The central question is therefore not only how much time is requested, but:

> **What continues operating during that time?**

## 4. Triggering event

On **17 July 2026**, the European Commission presented **COM(2026) 616 final**, a proposal to amend Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 in order to revise the European Union Emissions Trading System and its Market Stability Reserve.

The corresponding impact assessment, **SWD(2026) 616**, was published on the same date.

The Diego Memorandum does not claim that this proposal causes specific heatwaves, fires, health impacts, or territorial losses.

It records a different relationship:

> the proposal is being discussed inside a climatic and territorial base that is already altered.

The policy document is not evaluated as if it had appeared in a cold, stable, or materially neutral context.

---

## 5. Core distinction

### Political time

Political and institutional processes can:

- postpone deliberation;
- extend deadlines;
- introduce flexibility;
- negotiate exceptions;
- reopen files after recess periods;
- and set future dates for correction.

### Territorial time

During the same interval, the territory does not remain suspended.

Heat, drought, fire risk, ecosystem stress, infrastructure exposure, emergency mobilisation, health effects, economic losses, and social disruption continue to accumulate.

The governing asymmetry is:

```text
political postponement
→ active material trajectory
→ accumulated territorial cost
→ reduced preventive margin
→ more difficult next decision
```

The core thesis is:

> **What is postponed politically continues to accumulate territorially.**

---

## 6. Monitoring scale

The memorandum works at a **daily scale**, not as an hour-by-hour emergency log.

Each daily record asks:

1. What changed materially?
2. What remained active?
3. What new pressure appeared?
4. What institutional or political response occurred?
5. What remained publicly unmodified?
6. Did the available margin increase, remain stable, or decrease?

Operational details by the hour belong to emergency governance and are incorporated only when they materially alter the reading of the day.

Each daily record remains an independent piece. The September synthesis will compare the sequence without retrospectively rewriting earlier states.

---

## 7. Monitoring layers

The monitoring incorporates only significant and verifiable developments in the following layers:

### 7.1 Regulatory and political layer

- amendments, delays, withdrawal, or modification of the proposal;
- positions of Spain, other Member States, the European Parliament, the Commission, the Council, and political groups;
- explicit conditions, limits, or reservations;
- public latency: who speaks, when, with what language, and for how long a specific position remains unexpressed.

### 7.2 Material territorial layer

- heatwaves and thermal accumulation;
- drought and lack of rainfall;
- wildfire danger, active fires, evacuations, confinements, and closures;
- health, mortality estimates, and bodily exposure;
- agriculture, water, energy, transport, infrastructure, productivity, tourism, and fauna;
- territorial restrictions and emergency resource use.

### 7.3 Governance layer

- prevention;
- preparedness;
- emergency response;
- interterritorial coordination;
- European assistance;
- recovery announcements;
- allocation of responsibility;
- and whether the response modifies the trajectory or only manages its consequences.

### 7.4 Communication layer

- the distance between public narrative and material state;
- whether “contained”, “improving”, or “returning to normal” is presented before danger is materially closed;
- and whether communication creates a narrative reset that erases inherited conditions.

## 8. July 2026: initial sequence

### 17 July — the proposal enters

The European proposal enters the institutional calendar.

The monitoring interval begins.

### 22 July — situated civic notice

A situated citizen note is transmitted to institutions, organisations, representatives, and technical or legal teams.

Its purpose is not to impose a conclusion, but to place the proposal inside the actual climatic and territorial context and to request assessment from each actor’s field of responsibility.

### 23 July — reconsideration from 0 to 0

The internal reading is reformulated:

```text
0 → 0′ → 0″
```

A new formal cycle does not necessarily represent progress.

When the direction remains unchanged, each new starting point inherits:

- more accumulated cost;
- less margin;
- and greater irreversibility.

The transition from **0 to 1** cannot be declared by discourse or calendar. It requires material and verifiable change.

### 24–26 July — emergency scale changes

Large fires, evacuations, confinements, national emergency coordination, interregional mobilisation, and European assistance demonstrate that the system can unify its response.

However, that collective capacity appears mainly after consequences are already underway.

The distinction becomes:

```text
collective capacity to manage consequences
≠
collective capacity to reduce pressure beforehand
```

### 25 July — simultaneity becomes visible

Active fires, thermal accumulation, constrained emergency capacity, and continued eclipse promotion appear simultaneously.

The record does not claim automatic causality between these elements.

It documents that a territory already under extreme pressure continues to receive additional mobility, concentration, occupation, and economic expectation.

### 26 July — a limit is shown to be possible

The cancellation of an eclipse-related event in Huesca, after environmental and administrative guarantees could not be completed, demonstrates that a fixed calendar can be modified when a binding limit is recognised.

The question therefore changes from:

> Can an event be stopped?

to:

> Which activities and interests are willing to accept a territorial limit before the cost is transferred downward?

### 27 July — improvement does not equal recovery

The daily reading distinguishes:

```text
slower fire advance
≠
controlled fire

temporary meteorological improvement
≠
territorial recovery

end of one warning
≠
end of accumulated thermal pressure
```

A repeated evacuation and the announcement of a new heat episode reinforce the same principle: the interval does not preserve the territory in its previous state.

## 9. The eclipse as a situated test

The total solar eclipse of **12 August 2026** is not treated as a cause.

It is used as a fixed temporal marker crossing a variable territorial condition.

The astronomical event cannot be stopped. The human device around it can be modified:

- promotion;
- mobility;
- visitor concentration;
- observation points;
- parking;
- access to forest areas;
- service deployment;
- emergency resource reservation;
- attendance limits;
- relocation;
- reduction;
- or suspension.

The monitoring question is:

> **What material signal is considered sufficient to modify an already scheduled collective device?**

The absence of a disaster will not automatically demonstrate the absence of cost. The analysis must also include:

- prevention resources;
- restrictions;
- closed access;
- additional transport management;
- personnel deployment;
- emergency capacity reserved for the event;
- and pressure transferred to municipalities and services.

## 10. The collective 1

The **1** does not represent a leader, expert, saviour, or individual placed above the system.

It represents organised human capacity to assume responsibility and change direction.

A collective 1 can only be considered present when there are material and verifiable changes:

- public recognition of the trajectory error;
- immediate measures that reduce pressure and risk;
- change of purpose, not only improved efficiency;
- concrete, differentiated, and funded responsibilities;
- territorial repair and compensation for transferred costs;
- limits that prevent repetition;
- verification in the territory;
- and return of capacity to the places that absorb the impacts.

The sequence is:

```text
situated signal
→ shared reading
→ deliberation
→ coordinated decision
→ change of direction
→ territorial verification
```

The system already demonstrates substantial capacity to coordinate extinction, evacuation, confinement, assistance, and recovery.

The open question is whether comparable collective capacity can appear **before** the next pressure is added.

## 11. Base order and inverted order

### Base order

```text
TERRITORY
→ PEOPLE AND INSTITUTIONS
→ AI
→ NETWORK
→ TERRITORIAL RETURN
```

- The territory establishes conditions and limits.
- People and institutions deliberate and decide.
- AI helps read, compare, and relate.
- The network coordinates execution.
- The result returns capacity to the territory and is verified there.

### Inverted order

```text
INHERITED PURPOSE
→ NETWORK ACCELERATION
→ AI OPTIMISATION
→ HUMAN EXECUTION OR POSTPONEMENT
→ TERRITORIAL COST
```

In the inverted order, the territory becomes the place where costs are absorbed and where the next cycle begins from a worse base.

---

## 12. The recurring mechanism

The memorandum tracks the following recurrence:

```text
known conditions
→ public declaration
→ decision
→ postponement
→ continued material trajectory
→ accumulated cost
→ more difficult decision
→ new request for time
```

The complexity created by postponement can later become the justification for another postponement.

At that point, time ceases to be a resource for decision and becomes a mechanism for avoiding reorientation.

---

## 13. What this memorandum does not claim

The Diego Memorandum does not claim:

- that a single proposal, institution, or event explains the summer of 2026;
- that the eclipse causes fires;
- that every infrastructure produces the same territorial effect;
- that every delay is illegitimate;
- that internal institutional work does not exist when no public position has been located;
- that emergency coordination is unnecessary or ineffective;
- or that a final conclusion can be established before the monitoring interval is complete.

It distinguishes:

- evidence;
- source-based description;
- provisional classification;
- analytical relationship;
- and open question.
